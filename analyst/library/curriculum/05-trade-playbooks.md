# 05: Trade playbooks: setups with rules

A playbook is a repeatable situation with a known edge. Tag every trade with the
playbook letter in its thesis (e.g. "[A] PEAD long"). The weekly review then shows
which playbooks actually make money for you.

---
## A. Post-earnings drift (long or short)
**Situation:** a company reports a clear beat-and-raise (or miss-and-cut) and the stock
gaps strongly in that direction on heavy volume and *holds* the gap.
**Entry:** day 1–3 after the print, if the gap hasn't filled. Better if estimates are
being revised the same direction (news/web check).
**Invalidation:** price closes back inside the gap (below the pre-earnings close for longs).
**Exit:** trail behind the rising 20/50-day. Typical edge window is several weeks to the next quarter.
**Avoid:** gaps that fade intraday, one-off beats from tax or accounting items, low-quality
"beats" from cost cuts with revenue misses.

## B. Pullback in a leader
**Situation:** a stock in a strong uptrend (above rising 50 & 200-day, top-third
3–6 month momentum in its sector, fundamentals improving) pulls back on light volume
to its 50-day or a prior breakout level. RSI is around 35–45.
**Entry:** at or near support, ideally after a stabilizing day. Starter size, add on the
bounce.
**Invalidation:** decisive close below the 50-day *and* the last higher low.
**Exit:** prior highs as a first target. Hold a core while the trend persists.
**Avoid:** pullbacks driven by bad news (that's a thesis problem, not a pullback).

## C. Base breakout
**Situation:** a stock consolidates in a tight range for weeks or months near its highs,
then breaks out on 1.5–2× average volume, ideally on news or a sector move.
**Entry:** breakout day or first retest of the breakout level.
**Invalidation:** back inside the base for 2+ closes.
**Exit:** trail. Breakouts that work tend to work quickly. If it's flat after ~2 weeks, cut.

## D. Broken story short
**Situation:** guidance cut or structural problem (losing share, margin collapse, failed
product, regulatory hit), the stock is below a falling 50-day and 200-day, estimates are
falling, and the sector is weak.
**Entry:** on a failed rally toward the falling 50-day or prior breakdown level, *not* after a
capitulation flush.
**Invalidation:** reclaiming the 50-day on volume, or a positive catalyst.
**Exit:** cover in stages into high-volume flushes. Don't wait for zero.
**Avoid:** heavily shorted, small-float, meme-prone names. Avoid shorting ahead of a likely
takeover (beaten-down quality names attract bids).

## E. Oversold quality bounce (short-term mean reversion)
**Situation:** a high-quality large cap in a long-term uptrend (above its 200-day) drops
sharply over 3–5 days on market-wide or non-thesis news, RSI below 30.
**Entry:** after selling exhaustion (high-volume down day followed by an inside or up day).
**Invalidation:** new low after entry.
**Exit:** quick, at a bounce back to the 20-day average or a ~5–8% gain. This is a trade, not an investment.
**Avoid:** drops caused by company-specific bad news.

## F. Sector rotation / regime trade
**Situation:** the macro read (lesson 01) shifts, e.g. yields falling with growth OK (go
long rate-sensitive growth, small caps), oil spiking (long XLE, short airlines/transports),
defensives leading (rotate toward XLP/XLV/XLU, trim cyclicals).
**Instrument:** sector ETF for a clean expression, or the strongest one or two names in the sector.
**Invalidation:** the macro driver reverses (e.g. yields back above the level that triggered it).

## G. Relative value pair (market-neutral)
**Situation:** two similar companies diverge for a reason you think is wrong, or one has a
clearly better trajectory (e.g. share gainer vs share loser).
**Structure:** long the winner and short the loser in roughly equal dollar amounts. Your P&L
then depends on the spread, not the market.
**Invalidation:** the spread moves against you by your pre-set amount (e.g. 8–10%).

## H. Event / catalyst
**Situation:** a known upcoming event with a skewed outcome: investor day, FDA decision,
product launch, index inclusion, spin-off, activist involvement.
**Rule:** size as a binary bet (starter size) unless the asymmetry is extreme. Decide your exit
for *both* outcomes before the event.

## I. Defensive hedge
**Situation:** regime turning risk-off (lesson 01 signs) while you hold longs you want to keep.
**Structure:** short SPY/QQQ/IWM or the weakest sector ETF, sized to cut net exposure to the
target. Remove the hedge when breadth and credit heal.

---
### Pre-trade checklist (every entry)
1. Which playbook? What's the edge?
2. What does the market currently believe, and why am I different?
3. Regime and sector: with me or against me?
4. Catalyst and timeframe?
5. Invalidation level, and is the size consistent with it (lesson 04)?
6. Earnings date before my expected exit? Deliberate decision?
7. Correlation with my existing book?
8. Is this better than doing nothing?
