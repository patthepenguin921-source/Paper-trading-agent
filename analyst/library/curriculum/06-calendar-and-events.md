# 06: The calendar: when markets move and why

Check what's scheduled this week every Monday run (web search "economic calendar this
week" and "earnings this week"). Note it in your journal.

## Macro events (US, approximate timing; verify exact dates)
| Event | When | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **FOMC decision** | 8×/year, Wed 2:00pm ET + press conference 2:30 | Rate path. Dot plot quarterly (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec). Big intraday swings, often reversing the next day. |
| **FOMC minutes** | 3 weeks after each meeting | Occasionally move rate expectations |
| **CPI** | ~mid-month, 8:30am ET | The biggest scheduled market mover in inflation-sensitive regimes |
| **PPI** | Around CPI | Pipeline inflation |
| **PCE** | Late month | The Fed's preferred inflation gauge |
| **Jobs report (NFP)** | Usually first Friday, 8:30am ET | Growth and wages. Big rate/FX move |
| **Jobless claims** | Every Thursday 8:30am ET | Best high-frequency labor/recession signal |
| **ISM Manufacturing / Services** | 1st / 3rd business day of month | Cycle direction. New orders lead |
| **Retail sales** | ~mid-month | Consumer health |
| **GDP** | Quarterly advance estimate ~end of month after quarter | Backward-looking but can surprise |
| **Treasury auctions** (10y/30y) | Monthly | Weak demand pushes yields up and pressures stocks |
| **Fed chair speeches / Jackson Hole (late Aug)** | Ad hoc | Can shift the narrative |

Before a major event (CPI, FOMC, NFP), avoid initiating large new positions whose thesis
depends on rates. Be positioned *before* or react *after*, and don't gamble on the print itself.

## Earnings season rhythm
- Season starts ~2 weeks after quarter end (mid-Jan, mid-Apr, mid-Jul, mid-Oct) with
  the big banks (JPM, C, WFC, GS, MS, BAC).
- Weeks 2–4: mega-cap tech and most of the S&P. Peak volatility for individual names.
- Off-cycle reporters: NVDA (late in season), WMT/HD/TGT/LOW (mid-season, retail fiscal
  years end Jan), COST (monthly sales reports plus its own quarters), ORCL/ADBE/MU/NKE/FDX
  (fiscal years with odd quarters).
- **Read-throughs:** chip equipment and foundry results read into semis, hyperscaler capex
  guides read into NVDA/AVGO/AMD and power/utilities, banks read into the consumer and credit.

## Market-structure dates
- **Monthly options expiration (OPEX):** third Friday. Pinning and volatility around
  big strikes. Quarterly "triple witching" (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) brings heavy volume and
  index rebalances.
- **Index rebalances** (S&P quarterly; Russell reconstitution in late June): forced buying/selling.
- **Month-end / quarter-end:** pension rebalancing (if stocks rallied a lot, there may be
  selling into quarter-end) and window dressing.
- **Buyback blackout:** companies often pause buybacks in the weeks before earnings, so
  that support disappears.

## Seasonality (weak tendencies; never a thesis on its own)
- Nov–Apr has historically been stronger than May–Oct. September has historically been the weakest month.
- The "Santa rally" (last 5 trading days of Dec plus first 2 of Jan) tends to be positive.
- Tax-loss selling pressures the year's biggest losers into December, and they often bounce in early January.
- Midterm election years have historically seen weakness into the election followed by
  strong subsequent 12 months. Pre-election years have historically been strong.
- Holiday weeks have thin volume, so moves can be exaggerated and unreliable.
