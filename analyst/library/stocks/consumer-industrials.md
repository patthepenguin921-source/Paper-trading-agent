# Consumer & Industrials Dossiers

Reference briefs for US large caps in retail, restaurants/apparel, staples, aerospace & defense, machinery/conglomerates and transports. Written from background knowledge with a mid-2026 horizon. **Nothing here is a live quote.** Prices, multiples, guidance and recent prints must be pulled fresh before any trade (see each "Verify live" line).

---

## Primer: how these sectors trade

**Consumer discretionary drivers (XLY is AMZN/TSLA-heavy, so for retail/restaurants also watch XRT and RTH).** The US consumer is about two-thirds of GDP and spends out of *real* income: nominal wage growth minus inflation, multiplied by employment. Payrolls, jobless claims and real average hourly earnings set the baseline. Credit is the swing factor. Revolving credit growth, card delinquencies, student-loan repayment and BNPL usage tell you whether lower-income households are stretched. Wealth effects (equities, home prices) drive the upper-income cohort, which has carried a disproportionate share of spending since 2023. Housing *turnover* (existing-home sales), more than prices, drives home improvement: people renovate when they buy or sell, and mortgage-rate lock-in has suppressed turnover since 2022. When budgets tighten, consumers **trade down**: from restaurants to grocery, from Target to Walmart, from brands to private label, from big-ticket to small-ticket. Value leaders (WMT, COST, MCD's value menu) gain share while discretionary-heavy names (TGT, NKE, LULU) lose it. **Tariffs and input costs** (freight, cotton, beef, coffee, cocoa, labor) hit gross margin before retailers can reprice. Footwear/apparel sourced from Vietnam, China and Indonesia is especially exposed. Read comps by their parts. **Traffic** (transactions) shows real share and health. **Ticket** (average spend) can be inflated by price, so "comps up on ticket, traffic down" is a low-quality beat.

**Staples as defensives (XLP).** KO, PEP, PG, CL, MDLZ, PM and MO sell non-cyclical goods, carry low beta and pay dividends, so they behave partly like bonds. In the 2021-23 inflation they pushed through large price increases. The bill came due as **volume** declined, private label gained share and investors started asking about GLP-1 effects on snacking. The key quarterly split is organic sales = price/mix + volume, and the market now rewards volume recovery more than price. Staples earn heavily overseas, so a strong dollar is a translation headwind (CL, PM, KO, MDLZ most exposed) and a weak dollar is a tailwind. Commodity spikes (cocoa for MDLZ, resin/pulp for PG/CL, aluminum and sweeteners for KO/PEP) compress margins with a lag of two to four quarters, depending on hedges. Because staples are **rate-sensitive** bond proxies, rising long yields hurt them, especially the high yielders MO, PM and KO. Falling yields and risk-off tapes help them.

**Industrials (XLI).** The ISM manufacturing PMI (first business day of the month; above or below 50, and the new orders minus inventories spread) is the cycle clock for short-cycle names such as HON automation, CAT construction and FDX/UPS B2B volumes. Capex intentions, core capital goods orders (durable goods report) and regional Fed surveys confirm it. Long-cycle businesses trade on **backlog**, book-to-bill and price escalators. **Commercial aerospace** is supply-constrained: OEM build rates (737/A320 family, 787/A350), engine deliveries (LEAP, GTF) and supply-chain bottlenecks set OE volumes. **Aftermarket** (spare parts, shop visits) is the profit engine and tracks airline flight hours/ASMs, which have stayed resilient. **Defense** follows the budget (NDAA, appropriations, continuing resolutions, reconciliation add-ons), geopolitical demand for munitions and air defense, and execution risk on fixed-price development programs. **Agriculture** tracks net farm income, corn/soy prices, farmland values and dealer used-equipment inventory. **Freight** tracks goods consumption, the industrial economy and inventory cycles (Cass Freight Index, AAR weekly carloads, LMI, trucking spot rates).

**Regime behavior.**
- *Early expansion / recovery:* cyclicals lead. Machinery (CAT, DE), transports (FDX, UPS, UNP), discretionary retail (HD, LOW, TGT) and apparel (NKE, LULU) outperform, while staples lag as defensive premia unwind.
- *Late-cycle expansion with sticky inflation:* pricing-power names do well (COST, WMT, aerospace aftermarket, defense primes, railroads with pricing). Restaurants and staples see margin pressure from input costs.
- *Slowdown:* transports usually turn first (they lead ISM). Trade-down beneficiaries (WMT, COST, MCD) and staples gain relative strength. Discretionary/apparel and short-cycle industrials de-rate.
- *Recession:* staples, tobacco and WMT/COST outperform in relative terms. CAT, DE, FDX and UPS fall hardest but usually bottom *before* the earnings trough. Defense is acyclical.
- *Rising rates:* housing-linked HD/LOW and bond-proxy staples (MO, PM, KO, PG) suffer. Industrials with pricing power hold up better if the rate rise reflects growth.
- *Falling rates:* HD/LOW rally on the housing-turnover thesis, often before the data turns. Staples get a yield bid, and small-ticket discretionary benefits.

**Key data calendar:** ISM (1st business day), jobs report (first Friday), CPI (mid-month), retail sales (mid-month), UMich sentiment (twice monthly), existing-home sales (late month), durable goods (late month), AAR weekly rail traffic (Wednesdays), Boeing orders/deliveries (2nd Tuesday), USDA WASDE (around the 10th-12th). Retailers' fiscal Q4 ends in late January/early February, so "holiday quarter" prints come in February/March.

## Retail

### WMT: Walmart Inc.
- **Business:** The largest US retailer by revenue. Segments: Walmart US (about two-thirds of sales; groceries are well over half of US sales), Walmart International (Walmex, Flipkart/PhonePe, China, Canada; roughly a fifth) and Sam's Club US (roughly an eighth). Its growing high-margin profit pools are advertising (Walmart Connect, Vizio), memberships (Walmart+, Sam's) and marketplace/fulfillment services.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) US comps and e-commerce growth, especially whether gains come from higher-income households. (2) Operating income growing faster than sales thanks to ads, membership and automation. (3) Trade-down narrative strength in soft consumer tapes. (4) Tariff/pricing commentary. (5) Valuation: it has traded at a premium multiple closer to a tech/compounder than a grocer, which makes it sensitive to rotation.
- **Key numbers to check:** Walmart US comp ex-fuel (transactions vs ticket), e-commerce growth and profitability, global ad growth, gross margin rate, operating income growth vs sales growth, inventory, full-year sales/EPS guide (management guides conservatively and tends to beat-and-raise).
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends Jan 31. Reports mid-Feb (Q4 + annual guide), mid-May, mid-Aug and mid-Nov, before the open. Investment community meeting usually in spring. Monthly retail sales and CPI-food data. Leadership transition: John Furner succeeded Doug McMillon as CEO in early 2026. Listing moved to Nasdaq in late 2025.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: an omnichannel/ads flywheel lifts margins structurally while it keeps taking share in every income cohort. Bear: the premium multiple leaves no room for error, so any comp deceleration or margin hit from tariffs drives sharp de-rating even on a decent quarter.
- **Trading character:** Low beta (well under 1). Earnings-day moves are historically about ±2-7%; the February annual guide tends to produce the bigger moves. Extremely liquid. Strong momentum persistence in trending phases. Defensive in risk-off, but its high multiple means it can drop with growth names during valuation compressions.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** COST (closest peer), TGT (share donor), KR, DG/DLTR, BJ, AMZN. Sits in XLP (consumer staples) and the retail ETFs XRT/RTH.
- **Long setups:** Consumer-slowdown/trade-down regimes. Pullbacks to rising long-term moving averages after a "guide-conservative" sell-off. Pairs: long WMT / short TGT when discretionary weakens.
- **Short setups:** Rare as an outright short. Consider it after a parabolic run into earnings with multiple stretched versus COST/history, or in a risk-on rotation out of defensives. Prefer it as a funding short in a high-beta-on rotation.
- **Verify live:** Price, P/E versus its own history and COST, latest comps and guide, tariff commentary, CEO transition execution, ex-dividend date.

### COST: Costco Wholesale Corp.
- **Business:** Membership warehouse clubs, about 900 warehouses globally with most in US/Canada. Merchandise is sold near cost (gross margin around 11-13%). Membership fees are a small share of revenue but a large share of operating income. Mix: food & sundries and fresh are about half or more, with non-foods, gas and ancillary (pharmacy, optical, travel) the rest. E-commerce is growing faster than warehouses.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Monthly comparable sales (ex-gas, ex-FX). (2) Membership fee income, renewal rates (historically about 90%+ in US/Canada) and paid member growth. (3) Fee increases, historically about every 5-6 years with the last one in September 2024. (4) Special dividends. (5) Valuation: it trades at one of the richest multiples in retail, so it is sensitive to growth-to-value rotation.
- **Key numbers to check:** Comps ex-gas/FX by region, traffic vs ticket, digital comps, membership fee income growth, renewal rates, executive membership penetration, gross margin ex-gas, new warehouse openings.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends around the Sunday nearest Aug 31. Q1-Q3 are 12-week quarters and Q4 is 16 weeks. Earnings come in mid-Dec, early March, late May and late Sept, after the close (Thursday). Historically it publishes monthly sales results shortly after month-end, the most-watched monthly datapoint in retail. Watch also for gasoline price swings that distort headline comps.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: compounding member growth, rising renewal rates and digital growth give near-annuity earnings with optionality from fee hikes and special dividends. Bear: the multiple prices perfection, any comp deceleration or renewal dip can trigger a large de-rate, and fee-hike benefits are lapping.
- **Trading character:** Low-to-moderate beta. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±2-5%, and monthly sales prints can move it 1-3%. Very liquid. Strong trend-follower over months. In sharp risk-off episodes it can drop with high-multiple growth even though the fundamentals are defensive.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** WMT, BJ, TGT, AMZN. In XLY/XLP depending on classification (verify current GICS). Retail ETFs XRT/RTH.
- **Long setups:** A dip after a "not good enough" print with comps intact. Momentum entries ahead of a fee-hike anniversary or special-dividend speculation. Defensive rotations.
- **Short setups:** Only tactically: a stretched multiple combined with decelerating monthly comps, or a rotation into cyclicals/value. Better as the short leg of a pair (short COST / long BJ or WMT on valuation spread).
- **Verify live:** Price and P/E vs history, latest monthly comps (confirm monthly reporting cadence still applies), renewal rates, any fee-hike or special-dividend announcement, fiscal calendar dates.

### HD: The Home Depot
- **Business:** The largest home improvement retailer, with about 2,300+ stores. Sales are roughly half Pro (contractors) and half DIY. The SRS Distribution acquisition (2024) added specialty trade distribution (roofing, pool, landscaping) and the GMS acquisition (2025) added drywall/ceilings distribution, deepening the Pro push.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Housing turnover and mortgage rates: it trades as a rate/housing proxy and often moves on 10-year yield direction. (2) Comps, especially big-ticket (>$1,000) transactions, which signal project demand. (3) Pro vs DIY momentum and integration of SRS/GMS. (4) Operating margin trajectory (acquisitions dilute margins). (5) Weather and storm-related demand.
- **Key numbers to check:** Comp sales, comp transactions vs average ticket, big-ticket comps, Pro vs DIY commentary, gross and operating margin, SRS/GMS contribution, full-year comp/EPS guide, inventory.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends around the Sunday nearest Jan 31. Reports Tuesday before the open in mid/late Feb, mid-May, mid-Aug and mid-Nov, usually the day before LOW. Investor day sometimes in December. Data: existing-home sales, pending home sales, housing starts, mortgage rates/MBA applications, remodeling indices (LIRA).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: an aging housing stock (median home age well over 40 years), large home equity and an eventual turnover rebound unleash pent-up repair/remodel demand, and HD's Pro distribution moat wins share. Bear: "higher for longer" mortgage rates keep turnover depressed, big-ticket stays weak and acquisition dilution plus leverage compress returns.
- **Trading character:** Beta around 1. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±2-5%. Very liquid. Strongly correlated with rate-cut expectations: it tends to front-run Fed easing cycles, then fade if the housing data doesn't follow. Mean-reverting in range-bound housing regimes.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** LOW (very high correlation), FND, SHW, MAS, BLDR, homebuilders (XHB, ITB). XLY. Inverse to the 10-year yield.
- **Long setups:** Rates rolling over (10-year and mortgage rates falling), early signs of turnover recovery, an improving big-ticket comp trend, and a post-earnings dip when guidance holds.
- **Short setups:** Rate spikes and back-up in yields, a rally pricing in cuts that don't arrive, guide cuts, weak big-ticket. Pair: short HD / long a non-housing retailer when yields rise.
- **Verify live:** Price, comps trend, mortgage rate level and direction, existing-home sales trend, guide, SRS/GMS integration and leverage, dividend date.

### LOW: Lowe's Companies
- **Business:** The #2 US home improvement retailer, about 1,700 stores, US-only after exiting Canada in 2023. DIY-heavier than HD, with Pro around a quarter-plus of sales. It is pushing Pro via 2025 acquisitions of Artisan Design Group (interior finishes for builders) and Foundation Building Materials (drywall/ceilings distribution). Also pushing online, appliances and installation services.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Same housing/rate drivers as HD, with more DIY and discretionary sensitivity. (2) Comps vs HD (the market watches the gap). (3) Operating margin execution under its productivity program. (4) Pro share gains and integration of the new acquisitions. (5) Buybacks/leverage (historically aggressive buybacks).
- **Key numbers to check:** Comps, transactions vs ticket, Pro comps, online growth, operating margin, full-year comp/EPS guide, integration costs and leverage.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends around late Jan/early Feb. Reports in late Feb, mid/late May, mid/late Aug and mid/late Nov, before the open, typically the day after HD (HD's print sets up LOW's reaction). Same housing data as HD.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: higher operating leverage to a DIY/housing recovery than HD, and the Pro acquisitions widen its TAM. Bear: DIY discretionary softness persists, the acquisitions add leverage and integration risk, and it lags HD in Pro.
- **Trading character:** Beta around 1, slightly higher than HD. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±3-6%. Very liquid. Trades as a higher-beta HD around housing/rates news. Moves heavily on HD's print the day before.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** HD, FND, SHW, homebuilders (XHB/ITB), XLY.
- **Long setups:** Falling mortgage rates, DIY stabilization, HD printing well the day before with LOW lagging (sympathy entry). LOW-over-HD pair when the DIY recovery is the thesis.
- **Short setups:** Rising rates, DIY softness, HD guiding down. Pair: long HD / short LOW when Pro outperforms DIY.
- **Verify live:** Price, comps, HD's latest print, acquisition integration and leverage, mortgage rates, dividend date.

### TGT: Target Corporation
- **Business:** A general-merchandise discounter with about 1,950 stores. Mix is roughly half discretionary (apparel, home, hardlines including toys/electronics) and half essentials (food/beverage, beauty, household). Strong owned brands. Its stores-as-hubs fulfillment model (drive-up, same-day) and Roundel ad business are differentiators.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Comps and traffic: Target has seen negative/weak comps through 2024-25 amid discretionary weakness, a DEI-related boycott and share loss to WMT/AMZN. (2) Gross margin swings from markdowns, shrink, freight and tariffs; it has a history of huge misses (e.g. the 2022 inventory shock). (3) Turnaround credibility under new CEO Michael Fiddelke (took over Feb 2026). (4) Valuation and dividend support (long dividend-raise history). (5) Tariff exposure on discretionary imports.
- **Key numbers to check:** Comps (stores vs digital), traffic vs ticket, discretionary category trends, gross margin rate, operating margin, inventory health, full-year EPS guide, Roundel and same-day fulfillment growth.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends around Feb 1. Reports in early March (Q4), mid/late May, mid/late Aug and mid/late Nov, Wednesday before the open, in the same week as WMT/HD/LOW. Investor/strategy days under new leadership. Back-to-school and holiday traffic data.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: depressed sentiment and multiple, any traffic turnaround or margin recovery under new leadership triggers sharp re-rating, and there is ample room for discretionary to cycle up. Bear: structural share loss to WMT/AMZN/COST, an identity crisis, and a value trap with tariff and markdown risk.
- **Trading character:** Beta above 1 for a retailer. Earnings-day moves are historically very large, about ±8-20%, among the biggest in large-cap retail. Liquid. Can gap violently both ways. Short interest has risen during the slump, so squeezes on "less bad" prints are possible. Prone to long drawdowns, then sharp relief rallies.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** WMT (often inverse on share narrative), COST, AMZN, DG/DLTR, BBY, KSS/M. XLY/XRT.
- **Long setups:** Washed-out positioning plus a traffic inflection, "less bad" guide, new-CEO strategy reset with credible targets, or a discretionary spending upturn. Options-style sizing given gap risk.
- **Short setups:** Trend-following short while traffic is negative and WMT is gaining share. Pair: long WMT / short TGT. Into a print after a relief rally without evidence of traffic recovery.
- **Verify live:** Price, comps/traffic trend, CEO strategy and any new targets, tariff exposure, short interest, dividend coverage, earnings date.

## Restaurants & apparel

### MCD: McDonald's Corporation
- **Business:** About 95% of its 40,000+ restaurants are franchised. Revenue is mostly rent plus royalties (a high-margin, real-estate-like model) plus company-operated restaurant sales. Segments: US (about 40% of revenue), International Operated Markets (UK, Germany, France, Canada, Australia and others) and International Developmental Licensed markets (including China and the Middle East).
- **What moves the stock:** (1) US comps and traffic, especially the low-income cohort, which has been weakening and boosts the importance of value (the $5 Meal Deal, McValue). (2) Systemwide sales growth and unit growth (aggressive opening plan toward 50,000 restaurants by 2027). (3) Franchisee profitability/value disputes. (4) International comps, Middle East boycott effects and FX. (5) Food safety episodes (e.g. the E. coli outbreak in Oct 2024).
- **Key numbers to check:** Global, US, IOM and IDL comps; systemwide sales growth; restaurant margins; G&A; net unit growth; digital/loyalty sales; EPS vs consensus; capex/opening guidance.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Reports early Feb, late April/early May, late July/early Aug and late Oct/early Nov, before the open. Menu/value announcements, investor day, monthly restaurant traffic data (Placer.ai, card panels) and peer prints (YUM, QSR, WEN).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the franchise real-estate model compounds earnings, and its scale wins in value wars as weaker chains lose share. Bear: low-income consumer weakness plus a GLP-1 overhang caps traffic, and value promotions squeeze franchisee economics.
- **Trading character:** Low beta (around 0.5-0.8). Earnings-day moves are historically about ±2-5%. Very liquid. Defensive in risk-off, behaves partially like staples. Mean-reverting in ranges and rarely trends hard.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** YUM, QSR, WEN, DPZ, CMG, SBUX. XLY (but behaves more defensively). Correlates with XLP during risk-off.
- **Long setups:** Consumer slowdown with a trade-down to QSR. US traffic turning positive on value. Pullbacks to long-term support in a defensive rotation.
- **Short setups:** Negative US traffic with heavy discounting (margin pressure), a food safety incident, rising yields hurting defensives. Pair: short MCD / long a share-gaining QSR.
- **Verify live:** Price, latest US comps/traffic, value program reception, franchisee commentary, FX, dividend date (usually raised in the fall).

### SBUX: Starbucks Corporation
- **Business:** Coffeehouses, about 40,000 globally, roughly half company-operated (the US and China are company-operated heavy). Segments: North America (about three-quarters of revenue), International and Channel Development (the Nestlé Global Coffee Alliance). In 2025 it agreed to sell a majority stake in its China retail operations to Boyu Capital, forming a JV.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) The "Back to Starbucks" turnaround under CEO Brian Niccol (since Sept 2024): US comps and especially transactions. (2) Operating margin restoration versus heavy labor/service investment (the Green Apron service model). (3) China competition (Luckin, Cotti) and the JV transition. (4) Arabica coffee costs and tariffs. (5) Store closures/restructuring and labor relations (Workers United).
- **Key numbers to check:** US comps (transactions vs ticket), China comps, operating margin (US and consolidated), store count changes, Rewards member activity, EPS vs consensus, any reinstated long-term guidance.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends around the Sunday nearest Sept 30. Reports late Jan, late Apr, late Jul and late Oct/early Nov, after the close. Investor days, menu simplification milestones, the China JV closing, labor negotiations.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: Niccol repeats his Chipotle playbook, traffic returns and margins recover from depressed levels to historical mid-to-high teens in North America. Bear: the turnaround takes longer and costs more (labor), the premium price point loses value-seeking customers, and margins stay structurally lower.
- **Trading character:** Beta around 1. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±5-10%. Very liquid. Trades on turnaround narrative with frequent sharp reversals. "Show me" stock: hope rallies fade without traffic proof.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** MCD, CMG, DPZ, consumer-discretionary ETF XLY. Coffee futures (inverse, on cost). China consumer proxies.
- **Long setups:** First evidence of positive US transactions, margin trough confirmed, low expectations into a print.
- **Short setups:** A rally pricing in a turnaround ahead of data, with traffic still negative and margins guided lower. Coffee cost spike. Labor disruption.
- **Verify live:** Price, latest US comps/transactions, margin guide, China JV status, coffee prices, union negotiations, dividend coverage.

### NKE: Nike Inc.
- **Business:** The global athletic footwear/apparel leader. Nike Brand is roughly two-thirds footwear, about a third apparel, plus equipment, with Jordan Brand inside it. Converse is separate and small. Geographies: North America (about 40%+), EMEA, Greater China and APLA. Channels: wholesale (about 55-60%) and Nike Direct (stores and digital).
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Turnaround under CEO Elliott Hill (since Oct 2024): rebuilding wholesale relationships (Foot Locker, DICK'S), cleaning up oversupplied classic franchises (AF1, Dunk, Jordan 1), and launching performance/running innovation. (2) Gross margin recovery from discounting and tariffs; most production is in Vietnam, China and Indonesia. (3) Greater China weakness. (4) Share loss to On, Hoka (DECK), Adidas and New Balance. (5) Inventory health and future-orders commentary.
- **Key numbers to check:** Revenue by geography and channel, gross margin (bps change and the guide), inventory growth vs sales, Nike Direct vs wholesale growth, digital trend, next-quarter revenue/GM guide, tariff cost quantification.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends May 31. Reports late June (Q4 and fiscal-year outlook), late Sept, mid/late Dec and mid/late March, after the close. Product launches, major sporting events (Olympics, World Cup 2026), peer prints (ADDYY, DECK, ONON, FL, DKS).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the brand is still unmatched in scale/marketing, the inventory reset is behind it, and margin recovery from trough levels gives large EPS leverage. Bear: structural share loss in running/lifestyle, tariff costs, and China plus a long wholesale rebuild mean multiple years of sub-par margins.
- **Trading character:** Beta around 1, higher during the turnaround. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±6-15%. Very liquid. Long downtrend since 2021 with sharp bear-market rallies. Guide commentary on the call often reverses the initial after-hours move.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** ADDYY, DECK, ONON, LULU, UA, FL, DKS. XLY. Sensitive to China consumer data and US-Asia tariff headlines.
- **Long setups:** Gross margin inflection guided, inventory clean, wholesale order book growing. Tariff relief headlines. Washed-out sentiment into a print.
- **Short setups:** Tariff escalation on Vietnam/China/Indonesia, renewed inventory build, China deterioration. A rally into a print without evidence of GM recovery.
- **Verify live:** Price, latest GM and revenue guide, tariff status and mitigation, China trends, inventory, earnings date.

### CMG: Chipotle Mexican Grill
- **Business:** Fast-casual Mexican chain with about 3,800+ restaurants, almost all company-owned (a few international partnerships, e.g. the Middle East). Revenue is restaurant sales plus delivery. Growth is driven by new units (about 8-10% annual unit growth, most with Chipotlanes) and comps. Did a 50-for-1 stock split in June 2024.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Comps and especially transactions. Comps went negative in 2025 as younger/lower-income diners pulled back. (2) Restaurant-level margin, driven by beef, avocado, dairy, labor, menu pricing and throughput. (3) Unit growth pace. (4) Execution under CEO Scott Boatwright after Brian Niccol left for SBUX in Aug 2024. (5) Premium valuation that historically priced in high growth.
- **Key numbers to check:** Comps (transactions vs ticket), restaurant-level margin, new openings and Chipotlane share, full-year comp guide, food cost percentage, labor percentage, digital mix.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Reports early Feb, late April, late July and late Oct, after the close. Menu price increases, LTO launches (e.g. smoked brisket, chicken al pastor), restaurant traffic trackers, peer prints (SBUX, MCD, CAVA, SG).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: best-in-class unit economics and a long runway (target of 7,000 North American units), with a traffic recovery plus throughput gains restoring premium growth. Bear: traffic weakness is structural (value competition, younger consumer squeeze), margins compress on food inflation, and the multiple de-rates to a normal restaurant.
- **Trading character:** Beta above 1. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±5-12%. Very liquid post-split. A momentum name in good times, a de-rating story when comps turn. Sensitive to card-data/traffic-tracker reports mid-quarter.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** CAVA, SG, SBUX, MCD, WING, XLY.
- **Long setups:** Comps re-accelerating, traffic positive, de-rated multiple, a successful LTO cycle.
- **Short setups:** Negative transactions with margin guide cuts. Rallies into prints without traffic evidence. Food-cost (beef/avocado) spikes.
- **Verify live:** Price, comps/traffic trend, margin guide, beef/avocado costs, unit-growth guidance, earnings date.

### LULU: Lululemon Athletica
- **Business:** Premium athletic apparel. By product, women's is about 60%+, men's about a quarter, the rest accessories/footwear. By geography, the Americas about 70% (the US is weakening), China Mainland about 15% and growing fast, and the rest of world. Direct-to-consumer heavy (stores + e-commerce), with high gross margins (high-50s historically).
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Americas comps: the US has stagnated amid competition (Alo, Vuori, Athleta, Nike) and product newness issues. (2) China growth. (3) Gross margin hit from tariffs and the end of de minimis (US e-commerce shipments from Canada), plus markdowns. (4) Leadership/governance: a CEO transition after Calvin McDonald's announced departure, plus founder/activist pressure (verify). (5) Low valuation versus history, which attracts value and activists.
- **Key numbers to check:** Americas comps vs China growth, total comps, gross margin bps change, operating margin, inventory growth, next-quarter and full-year revenue/EPS guide, tariff cost quantification.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends around late Jan/early Feb. Reports late March (Q4), early June, late Aug/early Sept and early Dec, after the close. Holiday update sometimes in January (ICR conference). Product launches, CEO announcements, activist news.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: a strong brand at a de-rated multiple, China and international growth plus a US product refresh and new leadership drive a re-rating, possibly helped by activists. Bear: US brand fatigue is structural, competition is intense and tariffs/markdowns compress margins for years.
- **Trading character:** Beta above 1. Earnings-day moves are historically very large, about ±8-20%. Liquid. Persistent downtrend since early 2024 with violent relief rallies. Headline-driven by leadership/activist news.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** NKE, ONON, DECK, UAA, GPS (Athleta), XLY. China consumer data.
- **Long setups:** Washed-out multiple plus a catalyst (new CEO, activist settlement, US comp stabilization), a "less bad" guide.
- **Short setups:** Continued negative US comps plus tariff margin hits. Relief rallies without fundamentals. Guide cuts.
- **Verify live:** Price, CEO and activist situation, latest Americas/China comps, GM guide, tariff/de minimis impact, earnings date.

## Staples

### KO: The Coca-Cola Company
- **Business:** Asset-light concentrate model: it sells syrup/concentrate to independent bottlers (Coca-Cola HBC, FEMSA, Coca-Cola Europacific and others), plus some company-owned bottling being steadily refranchised. Brands: Coca-Cola, Sprite, Fanta, Powerade, Smartwater, Fairlife, Costa. About 60%+ of revenue and more of profits come from outside North America.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Organic revenue growth split into price/mix and unit case volume (the market wants positive volume). (2) FX translation, a large drag when the dollar is strong, especially from EM currencies like the Argentine peso. (3) Comparable EPS and its guide. (4) Rates: it trades as a bond proxy. (5) Defensive rotation flows.

## Transports

### UPS: United Parcel Service
- **Business:** The global parcel delivery leader. Segments: US Domestic Package (about two-thirds of revenue), International Package (about 20%; high margin) and Supply Chain Solutions (forwarding, logistics, healthcare logistics including the Andlauer acquisition). Its unionized workforce (Teamsters contract through 2028) gives it a high fixed-cost base.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) US domestic volume and revenue per piece. UPS is deliberately cutting Amazon volume by about half by mid-2026 ("glide-down") to improve yield, at the cost of volume. (2) Network reconfiguration savings: building closures, automation, headcount reductions. (3) Operating margin, especially US domestic. (4) International trade flows, tariffs, and the end of de minimis. (5) Dividend sustainability (payout vs FCF) and capital allocation.
- **Key numbers to check:** US domestic average daily volume, revenue per piece, US domestic adjusted operating margin, international margin, cost-savings delivered, FCF vs dividend, full-year revenue/margin guide.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Reports late Jan, late April, late July and late Oct, before the open. Peak season (Nov-Dec) commentary, FedEx prints (a read-across), USPS changes, trade policy, ISM and retail sales.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the Amazon glide-down and network overhaul lift margins and returns structurally, the stock is cheap vs history, and the high dividend pays you to wait. Bear: secular volume loss to Amazon's in-house logistics, union cost inflation, weak industrial/B2B demand and FCF pressure risk a dividend cut.
- **Trading character:** Beta around 1. Earnings-day moves are historically large, about ±5-15%. Very liquid. In a multi-year downtrend since 2022 with sharp bear rallies. Yield-supported but vulnerable to dividend-cut fears.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** FDX (high), AMZN, XPO, CHRW, EXPD. IYT (transport ETF), XLI.
- **Long setups:** Evidence of margin improvement from the network overhaul, an industrial cycle upturn, washed-out sentiment with dividend coverage intact.
- **Short setups:** Volume declines without margin offset, FCF short of the dividend, tariffs/trade disruption hurting international, FedEx warnings.
- **Verify live:** Price, dividend yield and coverage, latest volume/margin trends, Amazon glide-down progress, guidance, tariff/de minimis impacts.

### FDX: FedEx Corporation
- **Business:** Express (air, international), Ground (domestic parcel; now merged into one network as Federal Express Corporation under the "One FedEx"/Network 2.0 plan), Freight (LTL; planned spin-off as a separate public company by June 2026) and FedEx Dataworks. It has heavier B2B and industrial exposure than UPS.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Cost-out delivery: the DRIVE program and Network 2.0 integration savings. (2) Industrial economy and B2B volumes (ISM-sensitive). (3) International export trends, trade/tariffs and de minimis changes. (4) Freight spin value (LTL multiples are high). (5) Yield/pricing discipline vs volume. The USPS air contract ended Sept 2024.
- **Key numbers to check:** Adjusted operating margin by segment, average daily packages, yield, cost savings realized vs target, full-year EPS guide, capex, FCF, Freight spin details.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** The fiscal year ends May 31. Reports late June (Q4 + annual guide), mid-to-late Sept, mid-to-late Dec and mid-to-late March, after the close. Investor day, the Freight spin-off, ISM, trade headlines, peak season, UPS prints.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: structural cost-out drives margin expansion even in a flat volume environment, and the Freight separation unlocks value. Bear: industrial weakness and global trade disruption hit volumes, cost savings get competed away, and the integration stumbles.
- **Trading character:** Beta above 1. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±5-15%. Liquid. A macro/cycle bellwether whose prints move the transport sector and sometimes the market (read-through to global trade). Momentum swings around guidance.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** UPS, ODFL, XPO, SAIA, EXPD, CHRW. IYT, XLI. ISM.
- **Long setups:** ISM upturn, cost savings beating plan, trade-policy relief, the Freight spin catalyst.
- **Short setups:** ISM downturn, a trade war escalation, a missed cost-savings target, a guide cut.
- **Verify live:** **Whether FedEx Freight has spun off and how the remaining FDX is structured.** Price, guide, ISM trend, tariff/trade policy.

### UNP: Union Pacific Corporation
- **Business:** The largest US railroad by revenue, covering the western two-thirds of the US. Freight revenue mix (rough): bulk (coal, grain, fertilizer, food/refrigerated), industrial (chemicals, plastics, metals, forest products, energy) and premium (intermodal containers, autos). Strong Mexico gateway exposure. In July 2025 it agreed to acquire Norfolk Southern in a large cash-and-stock deal to form the first US transcontinental railroad, subject to Surface Transportation Board (STB) approval.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) The Norfolk Southern merger: STB review process, conditions, timeline and approval odds. (2) Operating ratio (OR; lower is better) and productivity under CEO Jim Vena's precision-railroading approach. (3) Volumes: AAR weekly carloads, intermodal (tied to imports/consumer goods), coal (tied to natgas prices and utility demand). (4) Pricing above inflation. (5) Trade/Mexico/tariff policy.
- **Key numbers to check:** Operating ratio, volumes by commodity group, revenue per car, price-vs-inflation commentary, EPS growth guide, service metrics (train speed, terminal dwell), merger timeline updates.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Reports Thursday before the open in the third or fourth week of Jan, April, July and Oct. AAR weekly rail traffic (Wednesdays), STB merger milestones, tariff/trade developments, natgas prices for coal.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the transcontinental merger creates a unique network with cost and revenue synergies, plus continued OR improvement and pricing power. Bear: merger rejection or onerous conditions, high integration risk, weak industrial and import volumes, coal secular decline.
- **Trading character:** Low-to-moderate beta (around 0.9-1). Earnings-day moves are historically about ±2-5%. Very liquid. Quality defensive-cyclical. Merger-driven headline risk on STB news.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** NSC (merger arb spread), CSX, CPKC, CNI. IYT, XLI.
- **Long setups:** Positive STB signals, OR improvement, volume recovery, an industrial upturn.
- **Short setups:** Merger rejection risk or onerous conditions, volume declines, trade disruptions (Mexico), service breakdowns.
- **Verify live:** Price, merger status and STB timeline, latest OR and volume trends, AAR weekly carloads, trade policy.
