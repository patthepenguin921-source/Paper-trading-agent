# Internet, Media, Telecom, Autos & Travel: Stock Dossiers

Covers: GOOGL, AMZN, META, UBER, SHOP, NFLX, DIS, T, VZ, TMUS, TSLA, F, GM, ABNB, BKNG.

Knowledge note: written from training knowledge (cutoff mid-2026). Any level, multiple, guidance figure or legal status below is historical context. Re-check everything time-sensitive before you trade.

---

## Primer: what drives these groups

**Internet & platforms (GOOGL, AMZN, META, UBER, SHOP).** Two engines dominate. The first is the **digital ad cycle**. Ad budgets follow nominal GDP and corporate confidence, but digital keeps taking share from linear TV and print, so these companies grow faster than overall ad spend. Ad revenue reacts quickly in both directions. In a slowdown, performance-ad platforms (META, Google Search) feel it within a quarter. They also recover first, because their measurable ROI lets advertisers shift money back fast. Watch Chinese e-commerce advertisers (Temu/Shein) and tariff and de minimis policy, retail sales, and peer prints: SNAP, PINS, TTD and RDDT often report before or alongside META/GOOGL. The second engine is **cloud and AI infrastructure** (AWS, Google Cloud). Here the market trades growth acceleration, backlog/RPO and, above all, **capex**. From 2024 to 2026 the megacaps traded on whether AI capex earns a return. Capex raises can be read as bullish (demand is strong) or bearish (free cash flow is being consumed), depending on whether revenue acceleration comes with them. UBER and SHOP are more tied to **consumer spending** and transaction volume (gross bookings, GMV). They carry higher beta and react more to rates and growth scares.

**Media & streaming (NFLX, DIS).** The industry has moved from "subscriber growth at any cost" to **subscriber economics**: ARPU, price increases, ad tiers, password-sharing crackdowns, churn, content amortization and operating margin. Linear TV (cable networks) is in secular decline from cord-cutting, so the market rewards the streaming mix and punishes linear exposure. Sports rights inflation is a structural cost headwind. DIS also carries a large **theme parks/cruise** business that trades on consumer discretionary spending and attendance. Industry M&A has become a live driver.

**Telecom (T, VZ, TMUS).** Mature, cash-generative, heavily indebted. T and VZ trade as **bond proxies**: high dividend yields make them sensitive to the 10-year Treasury yield, with falling yields helping and rising yields hurting. They outperform in defensive, risk-off tapes and lag in risk-on rallies. Fundamental drivers are **wireless competition**: postpaid phone net adds, churn, promotional intensity, and cable MVNOs (Charter/Comcast Spectrum and Xfinity Mobile) taking share at the low end. Fiber/fixed-wireless broadband, free cash flow coverage of the dividend, leverage and spectrum spend also matter. TMUS is a growth/compounder and trades less like a bond.

**Autos (TSLA, F, GM).** Deeply **cyclical**. The drivers are the US SAAR (seasonally adjusted annual sales rate), pricing and incentives, dealer inventory days, used-car prices (Manheim index), and **rates/financing**, since most new vehicles are financed and affordability tracks auto-loan APRs. The **EV transition** has turned into a story of capital destruction and write-downs for the legacy OEMs, especially after the US federal EV consumer tax credit ended (late Sept 2025) and emissions/CAFE penalties were loosened. **Tariffs** (Section 232 on autos/parts, steel and aluminum, USMCA rules, Mexico/Canada/Korea-built vehicles) are a large swing factor for GM and F. **UAW** contract cycles matter; the 2023 contract runs to April 2028. TSLA trades mostly on autonomy/robotaxi/AI optionality and Musk-related narrative, not on the auto cycle.

**Travel (ABNB, BKNG).** Leisure travel demand is resilient but discretionary. Watch TSA throughput, airline and hotel commentary (RevPAR), FX (BKNG is Europe-heavy), and macro sentiment. Both depend on Google for traffic. AI agents and Google's AI search are a perceived long-term disintermediation risk, and that weighs on multiples.

**Proxy ETFs.** **XLC** (Communication Services: GOOGL, META, NFLX, DIS, T, VZ, TMUS are core holdings). **XLY** (Consumer Discretionary: AMZN and TSLA dominate the weight; also BKNG, GM, F, ABNB). **QQQ** (Nasdaq-100: GOOGL, AMZN, META, TSLA, NFLX, BKNG, ABNB, SHOP, TMUS). Others: **IYZ** (telecom), **CARZ/DRIV** (autos/EV), **JETS** (airlines, as a travel read-through), **SOCL** (social media), **PEJ** (leisure). Note: UBER is GICS Industrials (sits in XLI), and SHOP is GICS Information Technology.

## Internet & platforms

### GOOGL: Alphabet Inc. (Class A; GOOG = Class C, no vote)
- **Business:** Google Services is the bulk of revenue. Search & other is the largest single line (roughly mid-50s% of total). YouTube ads are about 10%, Network (AdSense/AdMob) is a shrinking high-single-digit %, and subscriptions/platforms/devices (YouTube Premium/TV, Google One, Play, Pixel) are low-teens %. Google Cloud (GCP plus Workspace) has grown to a mid-teens % share and is now solidly profitable. Other Bets (Waymo, Verily) is small revenue with losses.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) The AI disruption narrative for Search (ChatGPT/AI answers vs Google's AI Overviews/AI Mode and Gemini monetization). (2) Cloud growth acceleration and backlog, plus TPU demand (including external TPU deals). (3) The capex trajectory against FCF. (4) Antitrust remedies. (5) Waymo progress and valuation marks.
- **Key numbers to check:** Search revenue growth (whether it is accelerating or decelerating), paid clicks/CPC commentary, YouTube ads growth, Cloud revenue growth and operating margin, Cloud backlog, total capex and next-year capex guidance, operating margin, buyback pace, Gemini user/usage metrics.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings late Jan/early Feb, late Apr, late Jul, late Oct. Google I/O (May). Cloud Next (spring). Gemini model releases. DOJ search case: a 2025 remedies ruling rejected forced Chrome divestiture but restricted exclusive deals; appeals are possible. DOJ ad-tech case: liability found in 2025, remedies phase follows. EU DMA/competition fines. Apple default-search payments.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: full-stack AI leader (TPUs, Gemini, Cloud, distribution), Search proves resilient and AI monetizes, and the stock re-rates from a discount multiple. Bear: AI chat erodes high-margin search queries, capex crushes FCF, and remedies weaken distribution.
- **Trading character:** Mega-cap liquidity, beta near or slightly above 1. Earnings-day moves are historically about ±4-9%. It trends for long stretches once a narrative turns (AI-loser to AI-winner swings were sharp in 2024-25). Short interest is negligible and there is no squeeze risk. In risk-off it falls with QQQ but is usually less hit than higher-multiple tech.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** META (ads), MSFT/AMZN (cloud), AVGO (TPU partner), QQQ, XLC. Pair ideas: GOOGL vs META on the ad share narrative, GOOGL vs MSFT on cloud/AI.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: Search growth holding or accelerating while the market prices disruption, Cloud reaccelerating, or a breakout after a favorable legal outcome. Short: evidence of query share loss (third-party traffic data, Apple Safari search commentary), a capex guide raise without revenue upside, or an adverse remedy ruling.
- **Verify live:** Current price/multiple versus peers, latest Search and Cloud growth rates, capex guide, status of the DOJ search appeals and ad-tech remedies, any EU decisions, Gemini competitive standing against OpenAI and Anthropic.

### AMZN: Amazon.com, Inc.
- **Business:** Online stores (1P retail) and third-party seller services (commissions and fulfillment) are the largest revenue lines. Subscriptions (Prime), Advertising (a fast-growing, high-margin line, roughly high-single-digit to ~10% of revenue) and AWS (high-teens % of revenue but the majority of operating income) follow. Segments: North America, International, AWS.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) AWS growth rate versus Azure and GCP, and whether capacity constraints (power, chips) are limiting it. (2) Retail operating margin (regionalized fulfillment, robotics, cost-to-serve). (3) Advertising growth. (4) Capex (very large, AI-driven) against FCF. (5) Q4 holiday guidance and consumer health.
- **Key numbers to check:** AWS revenue growth and operating margin, AWS backlog, ad revenue growth, North America and International operating margin, next-quarter operating income guidance range (Amazon guides op income, often conservatively), trailing FCF, capex commentary, Trainium/Bedrock commentary.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings late Jan/early Feb, late Apr/early May, late Jul/early Aug, late Oct. Prime Day (typically July), Prime Big Deal Days (October), re:Invent (late Nov/early Dec). FTC antitrust monopolization suit (trial slated for later in the decade). Project Kuiper satellite rollout costs. Anthropic investment marks.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: AWS reaccelerates on AI capacity, ads and retail margins expand, and FCF inflects as the capex cycle matures. Bear: AWS loses AI share to Azure/GCP/neoclouds, capex compresses FCF for years, and tariffs or a consumer slowdown pressure retail.
- **Trading character:** Beta about 1.2-1.3. Earnings moves historically about ±5-10%, sometimes more, because the AWS number and op income guide create binary reactions. Very liquid, momentum-friendly, tiny short interest. It sells off with QQQ in risk-off and is more consumer-sensitive than MSFT/GOOGL.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** MSFT, GOOGL (cloud), WMT/COST (retail), SHOP, UPS/FDX (logistics), XLY (AMZN is a top weight), QQQ.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: an AWS growth inflection with capacity coming online, or a pullback into earnings when expectations are reset low. Short: AWS decelerating while peers accelerate, a weak op income guide into holiday, or consumer/tariff stress.
- **Verify live:** Latest AWS growth vs Azure/GCP, capex guide, op income guide, FTC case status, tariff exposure commentary, current valuation.

### META: Meta Platforms, Inc.
- **Business:** Family of Apps (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger, Threads) produces roughly 98-99% of revenue, nearly all advertising. Reality Labs (Quest, Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses, metaverse) is small revenue with very large operating losses. WhatsApp business messaging and Threads ads are emerging lines.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Ad revenue growth, driven by impressions growth and price-per-ad, with AI ranking/recommendation and Advantage+ improving conversion. (2) Expense and **capex guidance**: AI infrastructure, superintelligence lab hiring, and very large data-center commitments. (3) Reality Labs losses. (4) Regulatory risk (EU DMA "pay or consent", youth-safety litigation). (5) The China advertiser mix and tariffs.
- **Key numbers to check:** Revenue guide range for next quarter, total expense guide, capex guide (current and next year), ad impressions growth, average price per ad, Family daily active people (DAP), FoA operating margin, RL operating loss, buybacks/dividend.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings late Jan, late Apr, late Jul, late Oct (after close). Meta Connect (Sept: glasses/AR). LlamaCon/AI model releases. FTC monopolization case (Instagram/WhatsApp; a late-2025 district ruling went in Meta's favor, so check appeal status). EU DMA decisions. State AG and school-district youth-harm trials.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: best-in-class AI-driven ad targeting compounds revenue in the high teens or better, WhatsApp/Threads monetization adds upside, and capex is earning a return. Bear: open-ended AI spend (data centers, talent) crushes FCF with no clear revenue, and Reality Labs keeps losing heavily.
- **Trading character:** Beta about 1.2-1.4. Earnings moves historically large, about ±7-15% (−20%+ in 2022, +20% in early 2024). Strong trend persistence. A capex/expense guide shock is the classic downside trigger even on revenue beats. Negligible short interest. Hit hard in risk-off ad-cycle scares.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** GOOGL, SNAP, PINS, RDDT, TTD, APP (ad tech), AMZN ads, XLC, QQQ.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: revenue guide well above consensus with expense discipline, or a post-earnings capex-scare selloff that stabilizes while ad growth stays strong. Short: decelerating ad growth combined with a capex/expense raise, or weakening ad checks (peer misses, Chinese advertiser pullback).
- **Verify live:** Latest revenue/expense/capex guides, ad growth trend, FTC appeal status, EU DMA fine status, AI model competitiveness, current valuation.

### UBER: Uber Technologies, Inc.
- **Business:** Mobility (rides, the largest profit contributor), Delivery (Uber Eats, grocery/retail), and Freight (small, low margin). Revenue is reported net of driver pay. Gross bookings is the key top-line measure. Advertising (in-app ads) is a growing high-margin line. Uber One membership drives frequency.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) **The autonomous-vehicle narrative.** Is Uber the aggregator AV fleets plug into (it has partnerships with Waymo in select cities and many AV developers), or is it disintermediated by Tesla/Waymo direct robotaxi apps? Tesla robotaxi headlines move UBER. (2) Gross bookings and trip growth. (3) Adjusted EBITDA margin and FCF/buybacks. (4) Consumer spending and insurance cost trends.
- **Key numbers to check:** Gross bookings growth (constant currency), trips growth, monthly active platform consumers (MAPCs), Mobility and Delivery EBITDA margins as % of GB, next-quarter GB and adjusted EBITDA guidance, FCF, buyback execution.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings early Feb, early May, early Aug, early Nov (usually pre-market). Product event (Go-Get, spring). AV partnership announcements. Tesla robotaxi expansion news. Regulatory items: driver classification, EU platform-work directive, FTC (Uber One subscription practices suit), insurance law reform.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: a profitable, FCF-rich network with a scale moat, and AVs lower its cost and get distributed through it. Bear: AV leaders build their own networks and turn Uber into a low-margin demand broker, at a time when the multiple prices in durable dominance.
- **Trading character:** Beta about 1.3-1.5. Earnings moves historically about ±5-10%. Very headline-sensitive to Tesla/Waymo news, with single-day drops of 5-10% on robotaxi events. Momentum-y. Low short interest. A high-beta casualty in risk-off.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** LYFT, DASH (delivery), TSLA (inverse narrative on AV days), GOOGL (Waymo), XLI, QQQ-like behavior despite its sector.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: a selloff on a competitor robotaxi headline while bookings and margins stay intact, or new AV partnership wins. Short: bookings deceleration, a credible competing AV launch in a top city, or rising insurance costs pressuring Mobility margins.
- **Verify live:** Waymo/Tesla robotaxi footprint and any Uber-exclusive deals, latest GB growth and guide, legal/regulatory cases, buyback authorization.

### SHOP: Shopify Inc.
- **Business:** Commerce platform for merchants. **Merchant Solutions** (roughly three-quarters of revenue: Shopify Payments, Shop Pay, Capital, Shipping, Installments) scales with GMV. **Subscription Solutions** (plans, apps, themes) is recurring and high-margin. Customers range from SMBs to enterprise (Shopify Plus, Commerce Components) and include offline POS and B2B. It is Canadian, reports in USD, and has been listed on Nasdaq since 2025 (Nasdaq-100 member).
- **What moves the stock:** (1) GMV growth (especially international, offline, B2B, enterprise wins). (2) Revenue growth against margin, i.e. FCF margin discipline. (3) Payments penetration and attach rate (take rate). (4) Consumer e-commerce health and tariffs/de minimis changes affecting merchants. (5) Agentic-commerce/AI narrative (checkout integrations with AI chat platforms).
- **Key numbers to check:** GMV growth, revenue growth, Gross Payments Volume penetration, MRR, attach rate, gross margin (Payments mix dilutes it), operating income, FCF margin and next-quarter guide (revenue growth range, gross profit growth, opex as % of revenue, FCF margin).
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings mid-Feb, early May, early Aug, early Nov (pre-market). Black Friday/Cyber Monday merchant sales data (Shopify publishes it in real time, a December catalyst). Biannual "Editions" product releases. Tariff/de minimis rulings.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the commerce operating system with a growing enterprise and international share, plus an AI agentic-checkout tailwind, delivering ~mid-20s% growth with FCF margins expanding. Bear: a premium multiple meets an e-commerce slowdown, margin compression from Payments mix, or a guide below whisper.
- **Trading character:** High beta (about 2). Earnings moves historically ±10-20%. A momentum/growth factor stock that rips in risk-on and gets punished in rate scares and risk-off. Short interest is modest. Correlates strongly with high-multiple software and the growth factor.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** AMZN, MELI, WIX, BIGC, PYPL, AFRM (BNPL partner), ETSY. Growth/software ETFs (IGV, ARKK). QQQ.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: a strong holiday GMV readout, a beat and raise with FCF margin held, or a pullback to trend support in a growth-friendly tape. Short: a rate spike or growth-factor unwind, decelerating GMV, or a guide implying margin compression after an extended run.
- **Verify live:** Current multiple vs growth, latest GMV/FCF trend, tariff/de minimis status for merchants, partnerships (AI checkout), rate backdrop.

## Media & streaming

### NFLX: Netflix, Inc.
- **Business:** Global subscription streaming (UCAN, EMEA, LATAM, APAC) with ad-supported tiers that are scaling. Revenue comes almost entirely from memberships plus a growing ad line. Expanding into live events/sports (NFL Christmas games, WWE Raw), games and experiences.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Revenue growth and **operating margin** guidance. Netflix guides to annual revenue and op margin and stopped reporting quarterly subscriber counts in 2025. (2) Pricing power (price hikes and churn reaction). (3) Ad tier revenue ramp. (4) Engagement (the semi-annual "What We Watch" report) and content slate. (5) **M&A**: Netflix's late-2025 agreement to acquire Warner Bros.' studio and streaming assets, contested by Paramount Skydance, adds financing, regulatory and strategic risk.
- **Key numbers to check:** Revenue vs guide, operating margin vs guide, next-quarter revenue/EPS guide, full-year op margin target, FX impact, FCF, ad revenue commentary, content amortization growth, buybacks.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Typically the **first megacap to report**: mid-Jan, mid-Apr, mid-Jul, mid-Oct (after close). Large live events. Price-increase announcements. Deal milestones (regulatory review, shareholder votes, financing).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the scaled winner of the streaming wars, with pricing power, ads and live content pushing margins toward media-best levels and compounding EPS. Bear: a large transformative acquisition dilutes returns and adds debt and integration risk, engagement plateaus against YouTube/short-form video, and the premium multiple compresses.
- **Trading character:** Beta about 1.2-1.4. Earnings moves historically ±7-15%. Because it reports first, it often sets tone for the megacap season. Strong momentum stock in 2023-25. A 10-for-1 split in late 2025 widened the retail base. Low short interest.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** DIS, WBD, PARA/PSKY, ROKU, SPOT. XLC, QQQ.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: a beat and op-margin guide raise, a deal-related selloff that overshoots once terms or regulatory clarity improve. Short: a deal escalation (higher price, more debt), a revenue guide cut or FX hit, decelerating engagement while the multiple is stretched.
- **Verify live:** Status and terms of the WBD transaction and any competing bids, antitrust review, current guidance, post-split price levels, valuation.

### DIS: The Walt Disney Company
- **Business:** Three segments. **Entertainment**: Direct-to-Consumer (Disney+, Hulu), linear networks (ABC, Disney Channel, FX, declining), and content sales/licensing (box office). **Sports**: ESPN linear plus the ESPN direct-to-consumer service launched in 2025. **Experiences**: domestic and international parks, cruise line, consumer products. Experiences generates the largest share of segment operating income.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Experiences demand: park attendance and per-capita spending, cruise expansion, the large multi-year parks capex plan, competition (Universal's Epic Universe opened 2025). (2) Streaming profitability (DTC operating margin trajectory). (3) Linear decline and ESPN's transition to DTC, sports rights costs. (4) Box office slate. (5) CEO succession and capital allocation (buybacks, dividend).
- **Key numbers to check:** Experiences operating income growth and forward bookings commentary, DTC operating income/margin (Disney de-emphasized subscriber counts from FY2026), ESPN advertising/affiliate revenue, adjusted EPS guidance for the fiscal year, FCF, buyback target.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Fiscal year ends around late Sept/early Oct. Earnings early Feb, early May, early Aug, mid-Nov. D23 events, film releases (Marvel/Pixar/Star Wars tentpoles), cruise ship launches, park openings, carriage disputes (e.g. YouTube TV blackouts), CEO transition after Iger (2026).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: parks are a cash machine with pricing power, streaming reaches mid-teens-plus margins, the ESPN DTC transition works, and the new CEO executes. Bear: a consumer slowdown hits parks, the linear decline accelerates, and content/sports costs keep streaming margins capped.
- **Trading character:** Beta about 1.0-1.2. Earnings moves historically ±4-10%. It has been a range-bound, mean-reverting name for years; rallies have tended to fade. Low short interest. Mixed behavior in risk-off: consumer-discretionary sensitivity via parks.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** CMCSA (Universal parks, NBC, Peacock), NFLX, WBD, PSKY, cruise lines (RCL, CCL), SIX/FUN, XLC.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: Experiences guide holding up against macro fear, streaming margin upside, or a bottom of the range with improving sentiment. Short: weakening park attendance or bookings, consumer slowdown data, disappointing new CEO strategy, a top-of-range fade after an earnings pop.
- **Verify live:** CEO and leadership status, current fiscal-year EPS guide, parks booking commentary, carriage disputes, box-office slate performance, sports-rights deals.

## Telecom

### T: AT&T Inc.
- **Business:** **Mobility** (US wireless, roughly two-thirds-plus of revenue and most of EBITDA). **Business Wireline** (legacy enterprise voice/data, declining). **Consumer Wireline** (AT&T Fiber, growing, plus legacy copper being retired). Latin America (Mexico wireless) is small. The strategy is fiber plus wireless convergence. It bought Lumen's consumer fiber business and EchoStar spectrum licenses in 2025.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Postpaid phone net adds and churn against competitive intensity. (2) Fiber net adds and broadband ARPU. (3) **FCF guidance** and dividend coverage. (4) Leverage (net debt/EBITDA target) and buyback program. (5) Interest rates (bond-proxy behavior).
- **Key numbers to check:** Postpaid phone net adds, postpaid phone churn, Mobility service revenue growth and EBITDA, postpaid phone ARPU, fiber net adds, Business Wireline EBITDA decline rate, FCF vs full-year guide, capex, buyback progress, multi-year EPS/FCF targets.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings ~third-fourth week of Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct (pre-market). Investor days. Spectrum auctions/purchases. Copper retirement regulatory approvals (FCC). The dividend has been flat since the 2022 WarnerMedia spin reset; it is declared quarterly.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: a de-levered, cleaner story with rising FCF, buybacks, fiber growth and a convergence advantage. Bear: a wireless price war (cable MVNOs, aggressive promos, VZ under new management) compresses ARPU, and legacy wireline declines faster.
- **Trading character:** Low beta (about 0.5-0.7). Earnings moves historically ±2-6%, larger on subscriber surprises. Mean-reverting, trades with the 10-year yield and defensive rotation. Outperforms in risk-off and lags in growth rallies. Very liquid, low short interest.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** VZ, TMUS, CMCSA/CHTR (broadband competition), utilities (XLU), long Treasuries (TLT). IYZ, XLC.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: falling yields plus defensive rotation, or a beat on postpaid adds/fiber with FCF reaffirmed. Short: rising yields, a promotional war (watch competitor price moves and holiday promos), weak net adds while the stock is near highs.
- **Verify live:** Current dividend yield vs the 10-year, FCF guide, latest net adds/churn, competitor promos, deal integration status (Lumen fiber, EchoStar spectrum), leverage.

### VZ: Verizon Communications Inc.
- **Business:** **Consumer** (wireless postpaid/prepaid plus Fios and fixed-wireless broadband) is about three-quarters of revenue. **Business** (enterprise, public sector, wholesale) is the rest and is declining. The Frontier Communications acquisition (fiber) closed after regulatory approvals and expands its fiber footprint.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Postpaid phone net adds, which have often been weak or negative, and churn. (2) **New CEO strategy**: Dan Schulman took over in late 2025, pushing cost cuts, large layoffs and a customer-retention reset. (3) Wireless service revenue growth and ARPU. (4) FCF and dividend coverage, leverage after Frontier. (5) Rates.
- **Key numbers to check:** Postpaid phone net adds, churn, wireless service revenue growth, broadband net adds (FWA plus Fios/Frontier fiber), adjusted EBITDA, FCF vs guide, capex, net unsecured debt/EBITDA.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings ~third-fourth week of Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct (pre-market, often the first telco to report). The dividend has historically been raised each September (a long streak). Strategy updates/investor day under new management. Spectrum deals.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: a cheap, high-yield stock with a turnaround catalyst. Cost cuts and fiber convergence stabilize subscribers and lift FCF. Bear: a value trap with subscriber losses and price-led competition, plus debt load and capex keeping it dead money.
- **Trading character:** Low beta (about 0.4-0.6). Earnings moves historically ±2-6%. A bond proxy and income vehicle, mean-reverting in a range. Risk-off haven. Low short interest.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** T, TMUS, CMCSA/CHTR, XLU, TLT, IYZ, XLC.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: yields falling, evidence the turnaround is stabilizing subscribers, a yield at the high end of its history. Short: rising rates, new-CEO promo aggression that hurts ARPU without fixing adds, or a guide cut.
- **Verify live:** Current guidance under new management, dividend yield vs the 10-year, Frontier integration/leverage, postpaid trend, layoff/cost program status.

### TMUS: T-Mobile US, Inc.
- **Business:** US wireless (postpaid is the bulk of service revenue, plus prepaid/Metro), a fast-growing fixed-wireless 5G home internet business, and fiber joint ventures (Lumos, Metronet). Deutsche Telekom is the majority/controlling shareholder. It closed the UScellular wireless acquisition in 2025.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Industry-leading postpaid net adds and share gains. Any sign of slowing is punished. (2) Service revenue and core adjusted EBITDA growth. (3) FCF and **shareholder returns** (large buybacks plus a dividend initiated in 2023). (4) Competitive intensity: VZ and AT&T aggression, cable. (5) Leadership: Srini Gopalan succeeded Mike Sievert as CEO in late 2025.
- **Key numbers to check:** Postpaid net adds (the company has signaled a shift in emphasis toward account-based metrics; verify the current headline KPI), postpaid phone churn, postpaid ARPA, HSI (broadband) net adds, core adjusted EBITDA and FCF guidance, buyback/dividend.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings late Jan/early Feb, late Apr, late Jul, late Oct. Un-carrier events. Capital markets days. DT/SoftBank share sales or block trades. Spectrum transactions.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the best network and value position, gaining share with operating leverage and huge capital returns, i.e. a compounding growth-telco. Bear: industry growth slows and competitors' promotions erode the net-add lead, and the premium multiple to T/VZ compresses.
- **Trading character:** Beta about 0.5-0.7, but it trades more like a quality compounder than a bond proxy. Earnings moves historically ±3-7%. Block sales by large holders (DT/SoftBank) can create temporary dips. Low short interest. Holds up in risk-off but can de-rate when growth leadership is questioned.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** T, VZ (pair trades: TMUS long vs VZ short on share gains), CMCSA/CHTR, XLC, QQQ (it is a Nasdaq-100 member).
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: share-gain confirmation and a guidance raise, a pullback on a block sale or competitor-promo scare without fundamental change. Short: a decelerating net-add lead, rising churn, a competitive price war where TMUS must respond, rich relative valuation.
- **Verify live:** Current KPI definitions, guide, competitive promo landscape, large-holder selling, capital return pace, leadership changes.

## Autos

### TSLA: Tesla, Inc.
- **Business:** Automotive (Model 3/Y dominate volume, plus S/X, Cybertruck, Semi; regulatory credits; FSD software) is the large majority of revenue. **Energy generation & storage** (Megapack, Powerwall) is growing fast with higher margins. Services & other covers used cars, Supercharging, insurance and service. Optionality: FSD, Robotaxi/Cybercab, Optimus humanoid robots, Dojo/AI compute.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) The **autonomy/robotaxi narrative**: geographic expansion of the robotaxi service (launched in Austin in 2025), unsupervised FSD milestones, regulatory approvals. (2) Musk himself: statements, political activity, attention split across xAI/SpaceX, and the very large performance pay package approved in late 2025. (3) Deliveries and auto gross margin (ex-credits), with price cuts and the end of the US EV credit hurting demand. (4) Energy storage growth. (5) Optimus timelines.
- **Key numbers to check:** Quarterly production and delivery report (released ~2nd day after quarter end, versus consensus), auto gross margin ex-credits, regulatory credit revenue (falling as emissions penalties weakened), energy storage deployments (GWh), opex and capex, FCF, FSD/robotaxi updates on the call.
