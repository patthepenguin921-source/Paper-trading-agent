# Tech: Semiconductors, Hardware & Software — Trading Dossiers

> Reference knowledge written from general market history, current to roughly mid-2026. Nothing here is a live quote. Prices, multiples, guidance, short interest, implied moves and regulatory status change fast. Check every "Verify live" item before sizing a trade.

## Sector primer

**What drives the group.** Large-cap tech is the biggest weight in US equity indices. It moves on four things at once: (1) the **AI capex cycle**, meaning hyperscaler (MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META, ORCL) and neocloud/sovereign spending on GPUs, custom ASICs, networking, memory and power; (2) the classic **semiconductor inventory cycle**, where PCs, phones, autos and industrial demand swing between restocking and destocking roughly every 3-4 years; (3) **rates and multiples**, because long-duration growth stocks (software, high-multiple AI names) are sensitive to the 10-year real yield; and (4) **geopolitics**: US export controls on China (AI accelerators, wafer fab equipment), Chinese retaliation (rare earths, antitrust probes, "buy domestic" directives), tariffs and Section 232 semiconductor investigations, and Taiwan risk through TSMC.

**Semis vs. software.** Semis are cyclical. Their earnings swing hard with utilization, so the classic rule is to buy when P/E looks high at the trough and sell when P/E looks low at the peak, especially in memory and equipment. AI has split the group. Compute and networking (NVDA, AVGO, AMD, MU via HBM) trade on hyperscaler capex. Analog and industrial (TXN) and handset (QCOM, AAPL) names still trade on the old cycle. Software trades on growth durability: net new ARR, cRPO, net revenue retention, seat trends and margin discipline (Rule of 40). Since 2024 the main software debate has been **"AI winner or AI victim"**. Seat-based SaaS (CRM, ADBE, NOW, INTU) faces worry that AI agents shrink seat counts and lower barriers to entry. Infrastructure, data and security (MSFT, ORCL, SNOW, PLTR, CRWD, PANW) is more often seen as an AI beneficiary.

**Key industry metrics to watch.**
- Hyperscaler capex guides (MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN/META earnings, late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct). This is the single biggest input for AI semis.
- TSMC monthly sales (around the 10th of each month) and quarterly guidance. CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity.
- DRAM/NAND contract and spot prices (TrendForce), HBM supply commentary from SK Hynix and Samsung.
- Wafer fab equipment (WFE) annual estimates; China's share of equipment revenue.
- SIA monthly global semiconductor sales; book-to-bill; distributor inventory days.
- PC and smartphone unit data (IDC, Counterpoint, Canalys).
- Software: cRPO growth, NRR, billings, guided net new ARR, IT budget surveys, and CIO commentary from consultancies (ACN results).

**How the group trades around earnings and macro.** Mega-cap reports move the whole complex. NVDA's report is a sector-wide event, and hyperscaler capex lines move semis more than the hyperscalers' own stocks. Options usually price larger moves than the stocks realize for mega-caps (AAPL/MSFT), while mid-cap software and high-multiple names (PLTR, SNOW, CRWD) routinely gap 8-20%. A "beat and raise but sell off" outcome is common when positioning is crowded; the guide and the second derivative matter more than the beat. On macro days (CPI, FOMC, payrolls) high-multiple software and SMH are the high-beta expressions, and a rise in real yields hits unprofitable or high-P/S software hardest. In risk-off episodes semis usually fall 1.5-2x the market. Low-beta names (CSCO, IBM, TXN, AAPL, MSFT) hold up better.

**Leaders/laggards dynamics.** Leadership is narrow and momentum-driven. AI-exposed names trend for months, and breaks in leaders (NVDA, AVGO) often mark sector tops. Rotations between AI winners and "AI losers"/value tech can be violent. Laggard catch-up trades (INTC, ADBE, QCOM) work only with a concrete catalyst. Watch for "DeepSeek-style" shocks: news that AI compute efficiency is improving fast or that capex is peaking can hit the whole AI-infrastructure basket by 10-20% in days, while software can sometimes benefit.

**Proxies and hedges.**
- **XLK** (Technology Select Sector SPDR): cap-weighted and very concentrated in NVDA/MSFT/AAPL/AVGO. It is the default hedge for large-cap tech longs.
- **SMH** (VanEck Semiconductor) is heavily weighted to NVDA and TSM. **SOXX** is more equal-weighted. Use SMH to hedge AI-semi longs, and SOXX/SOXL for broader semi beta.
- **QQQ** (Nasdaq-100) is the broad growth/mega-cap hedge. **IGV** is the software ETF and the cleaner hedge for SaaS names.
- Pair ideas: long a leader vs. short the ETF to isolate idiosyncratic alpha. For example, long CRWD vs. short IGV, or long MU vs. short SMH.

## Semiconductors & hardware

### AAPL: Apple Inc.
- **Business:** iPhone is roughly half of revenue. Services (App Store, Google search default payments, iCloud, Apple Music/TV+, AppleCare, Apple Pay) is roughly a quarter of revenue but a much larger share of gross profit. Mac, iPad and Wearables/Home/Accessories make up the rest. Greater China is a meaningful but shrinking share of sales.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) iPhone upgrade cycles and the narrative about whether Apple Intelligence or Siri revamps drive replacements; (2) Services growth and margin, plus legal threats to it (Google payment remedies after the DOJ search case, Epic/App Store anti-steering rulings, EU DMA); (3) tariffs and supply-chain moves (China to India/Vietnam), which feed gross margin; (4) China demand vs. Huawei and local brands, plus government subsidy programs; (5) capital return, since the roughly $100B/yr-scale buyback acts as a valuation floor.
- **Key numbers to check:** total revenue growth guide (Apple gives only a range, not line items), gross margin guide, Services growth rate, iPhone revenue vs. consensus, Greater China revenue, any tariff cost quantification.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** FY ends late September. Earnings come late Jan/early Feb (holiday quarter, the biggest), late Apr/early May, late Jul/early Aug, and late Oct/early Nov. WWDC is in early June (software/AI). The iPhone launch event is in September. Watch DOJ antitrust case milestones, EU DMA fines and tariff announcements.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the installed base of over 2 billion devices monetizes through Services, and an AI-driven upgrade supercycle plus new form factors (foldable) re-accelerate hardware. Bear: hardware growth is low single digit at a premium multiple, Services faces regulatory erosion, and Apple lags in AI.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.0-1.2, among the most liquid stocks in the world. Earnings-day moves are usually ±2-6%. It tends to trend slowly and mean-revert around large index flows. Short interest is very low, so squeeze risk is negligible. In risk-off it acts as a quasi-defensive mega-cap but is hit hard by tariff and China headlines specifically.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** QQQ, XLK. Suppliers include AVGO, QCOM (modems, declining), TXN, Skyworks/Qorvo, TSMC, Foxconn, and Cirrus Logic (very high AAPL dependence). GOOGL is linked through the search deal.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: tariff or China fear dips into support with an unchanged Services trajectory; strong iPhone checks into the September launch; favorable legal rulings on Google payments. Short: rallies into a launch event (classic "sell the news" pattern); new tariff escalation; adverse App Store or search-payment rulings; gross margin guide cuts.
- **Verify live:** current tariff regime on China/India-made devices, status of Google search payments and App Store litigation, the latest guide, Apple Intelligence/Siri timeline, China sales data, and the implied earnings move.

### NVDA: NVIDIA Corporation
- **Business:** Data Center is the overwhelming majority of revenue (historically about 85-90%+). It includes GPU compute systems (Hopper, then Blackwell/Blackwell Ultra, with Rubin next on the roadmap) and networking (NVLink, InfiniBand, Spectrum-X Ethernet). Gaming GPUs, Professional Visualization and Automotive/robotics are much smaller.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) hyperscaler and sovereign/neocloud AI capex guidance; (2) product transition execution (Blackwell to Rubin ramps, rack-scale yields, TSMC CoWoS capacity); (3) gross margin trajectory during transitions; (4) China export controls, since H20-class sales have been restricted, licensed and subject to revenue-share arrangements and Chinese pushback; (5) competition and perceived capex peaking: custom ASICs (Google TPU via AVGO, Amazon Trainium, Meta MTIA), AMD Instinct, and AI-efficiency shocks.
- **Key numbers to check:** next-quarter revenue guide vs. buy-side "whisper" (not just consensus), data center revenue and networking growth, gross margin guide, commentary on supply and visibility, China contribution, inventory and purchase commitments, and the size of the buyback.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** FY ends late January. Earnings come late Feb, late May, late Aug, and around the week before Thanksgiving in Nov. GTC is in March (roadmap), and Computex in late May/early June. Watch Commerce/BIS rule changes, hyperscaler earnings (about four weeks before NVDA), TSMC monthly sales, and large AI lab funding/compute deals (OpenAI and others).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: NVIDIA is the full-stack AI platform (CUDA, networking, systems), and annual cadence plus inference demand extend the cycle for years. Bear: customer concentration among a handful of hyperscalers that are building their own chips, circular financing in AI deals, and a capex digestion phase would compress both growth and multiple.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.6-2.0. It is the most traded US stock by dollar volume. Earnings-day moves are historically ±3-10%, with options often pricing about ±6-8%. It is a strong momentum stock with sharp 15-30% drawdowns on capex scares. Short interest is low as a percentage of float. In risk-off it sells off hard as a crowded long.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** SMH (large weight), AVGO, AMD, TSM, MU, SK Hynix, ANET, VRT, SMCI, DELL, CRDO and the AI-power complex. The hyperscalers are its customers.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: pullbacks to rising long-term moving averages after hyperscalers raise capex guides; into GTC/earnings when positioning is light; on resolution of China licensing. Short: (tactically) after a parabolic run into earnings with very high expectations; on evidence of capex cuts, order push-outs, or a gross margin step-down; on new export restrictions. Hedge with SMH rather than shorting outright.
- **Verify live:** the latest hyperscaler capex guides, China export status, current product ramp (Blackwell Ultra/Rubin) timing, gross margin guide, the implied move, and any antitrust probes (DOJ, China SAMR, EU).

### AVGO: Broadcom Inc.
- **Business:** two halves. **Semiconductor Solutions** covers custom AI accelerators (XPUs for Google TPU, Meta and other hyperscalers; a large OpenAI custom chip program was announced in 2025), Ethernet switching/routing (Tomahawk, Jericho), broadband, storage connectivity, and wireless RF (Apple is a major customer). **Infrastructure Software** is roughly 40% of revenue, mostly VMware (acquired late 2023) plus CA, Symantec enterprise and Brocade.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) the AI semiconductor revenue guide and the number of XPU customers or prospects; (2) multi-year AI serviceable-market targets for hyperscale customers; (3) VMware conversion to VCF subscriptions, which drives software growth and margin; (4) gross margin mix, since custom XPUs and rack systems dilute margin; (5) Apple RF content and any in-sourcing.
- **Key numbers to check:** next-quarter AI revenue guide, AI networking vs. XPU split, infrastructure software growth, consolidated revenue and EBITDA margin guide, free cash flow, and debt paydown.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** FY ends around late Oct/early Nov. Earnings come early-to-mid Dec, early-to-mid Mar, early-to-mid Jun, and early-to-mid Sep, typically on Thursdays after close. Hyperscaler events (Google Cloud Next, TPU announcements), OpenAI chip milestones and VMware customer/antitrust disputes (especially in the EU) also matter.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: Broadcom is the leading custom-silicon partner as hyperscalers diversify away from NVIDIA, plus the dominant Ethernet AI-networking supplier, with a cash-cow software business funding a growing dividend. Bear: customer concentration (Google especially), lumpy XPU program timing, gross-margin dilution, and a premium multiple leave little room for any AI guide shortfall.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.2-1.6, very liquid. Earnings-day moves are historically ±5-15%, with several double-digit gaps on AI guides. It trends with the AI trade, less violently than NVDA but with occasional big gaps. Short interest is low. It sells off with SMH in risk-off, while the software and dividend profile offers some cushion.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** NVDA, MRVL (custom ASIC competitor), ANET (Ethernet), TSM, GOOGL (TPU), SMH, CRDO/ALAB (connectivity).
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: into or after earnings when the AI guide steps up and new XPU customers are confirmed; on dips caused by sector-wide capex scares that don't touch AVGO's specific programs. Short: if the AI guide only meets elevated whispers or margin compression surprises; if a major XPU customer slips or shifts work to MRVL/in-house; VMware churn or legal headwinds.
- **Verify live:** the latest AI revenue guide and customer count, OpenAI and other program timelines, VMware renewal trends, debt level, the implied move, and Google TPU external-sales developments.

### AMD: Advanced Micro Devices
- **Business:** Data Center covers EPYC server CPUs and Instinct GPUs (MI300 family, then MI350/MI355, with MI400 and the "Helios" rack-scale system planned for 2026). Client covers Ryzen PC CPUs. Gaming covers Radeon and semi-custom console chips (Sony/Microsoft). Embedded is Xilinx FPGAs. Data Center is now the largest segment.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Instinct GPU ramp credibility and named customer wins; a large multi-gigawatt OpenAI agreement with warrants was announced in late 2025; (2) EPYC server share gains vs. Intel; (3) PC CPU cycle (Windows refresh, share vs. INTC); (4) China export rules on MI308-class parts; (5) gross margin progress.
- **Key numbers to check:** data center GPU revenue trajectory (AMD has been inconsistent about disclosing an explicit annual GPU figure), data center segment growth, next-quarter revenue and gross margin guide, client share, and long-term targets from analyst days.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** calendar FY. Earnings come late Jan/early Feb, late Apr/early May, late Jul/early Aug, and late Oct/early Nov. "Advancing AI" events (typically June), Computex, CES (January), and the Financial Analyst Day (one was held in Nov 2025) are the main events. Hyperscaler deployment announcements and OpenAI deployment milestones matter too.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: AMD is the credible second source to NVIDIA in AI accelerators with rack-scale systems and marquee customers, while still taking server CPU share; the earnings power by 2027 is materially higher. Bear: the GPU ramp keeps slipping vs. NVIDIA's annual cadence, ROCm software lags CUDA, and the stock trades on promises rather than delivered numbers.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.7-2.2, very liquid with a heavy retail and options presence. Earnings-day moves are historically ±5-12%, frequently negative even on beats because of high GPU expectations. It is prone to sharp narrative-driven rallies (deal announcements) followed by long fades. Short interest is moderate-low. It underperforms in risk-off.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** NVDA, INTC (inverse share dynamics in CPUs), TSM, SMH, SOXX, AVGO/MRVL (custom silicon competition), and server OEMs.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: after a capitulation fade when a concrete GPU deployment or customer announcement lands; ahead of analyst days that tend to reset targets. Short: post-event spikes on headline deals before revenue arrives; earnings where the GPU guide underwhelms; PC destocking signals.
- **Verify live:** MI400/Helios timing, OpenAI and other deployment status, China export licensing, current GPU revenue framing, the implied move, and the latest share data (Mercury Research).

### INTC: Intel Corporation
- **Business:** Client Computing Group (PC CPUs, largest segment), Data Center & AI (Xeon), and Intel Foundry (manufacturing for itself and hoped-for external customers). Altera (majority stake sold to Silver Lake) and Mobileye (majority owned, publicly listed) are partial holdings.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) foundry credibility: 18A yields and ramp (Panther Lake), 14A customer commitments, and any external anchor customer wins; (2) strategic and government involvement: the US government took roughly a 10% equity stake in 2025, and NVIDIA ($5B, with a co-development deal) and SoftBank invested; (3) PC and server share vs. AMD and Arm; (4) cost cuts and capex discipline under CEO Lip-Bu Tan (since March 2025); (5) gross margin recovery from depressed levels.
- **Key numbers to check:** gross margin guide, foundry operating loss trajectory, 18A yield/ramp commentary, external foundry customer announcements, client and DCAI revenue, free cash flow (it has been negative), and capex.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** calendar FY. Earnings come late Jan, late Apr, late Jul, and late Oct (usually Thursday after close). Intel Foundry Direct Connect (spring), CES and product launches also matter. Watch government (CHIPS Act terms, defense/secure enclave) and any foundry partnership news (Apple, AMD, Broadcom, and others are rumored at times).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: Intel is the only US-headquartered leading-edge logic foundry, has strategic national-security backing, and could be re-rated on a major external customer win; the asset value exceeds the price. Bear: process execution keeps lagging TSMC, CPU share keeps eroding, the foundry burns cash for years, and the dividend is gone.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.2-1.6. It is a heavily traded "turnaround story" with headline-driven gaps of 5-20% on rumors (foundry customers, government stakes, M&A). Earnings-day moves are historically ±5-15%, frequently negative. It is mean-reverting within ranges unless a real catalyst lands. Short interest is moderate, and it is popular with retail.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** AMD (inverse share narrative), TSM, AMAT/LRCX (as a foundry capex customer), SOXX, and arguably NVDA via its investment.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: on confirmed external 18A/14A customer commitments, government contracts, or a gross margin beat with credible 18A yields; pullbacks after rumor-driven spikes fade. Short: after unconfirmed-rumor spikes; on 18A delays, guide cuts or new equity dilution.
- **Verify live:** latest foundry customer news, government/strategic stake terms, gross margin guide, share count (dilution from investments), Altera/Mobileye stake status, and the implied move.

### QCOM: Qualcomm Incorporated
- **Business:** QCT (chips) is roughly 85% of revenue: handset Snapdragon SoCs and modems (the largest piece), plus Automotive (Snapdragon Digital Chassis) and IoT (PCs, XR, industrial). QTL (patent licensing) is a smaller, very high-margin business. It has announced data-center AI inference products (AI200/AI250) and acquired Alphawave for connectivity.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Apple modem roll-off, as Apple's in-house C1 modem phases out QCOM content over several years; (2) Android flagship cycles and China handset demand (Xiaomi, Vivo, Oppo, Honor; Huawei has exited); (3) diversification proof: automotive growth, Windows-on-Arm PC share, data-center wins; (4) handset share vs. MediaTek; (5) licensing disputes (Arm litigation, Chinese OEM renewals).
- **Key numbers to check:** QCT handset revenue guide, automotive and IoT growth, QTL revenue, EPS guide (it guides a range), gross margin, and commentary on Apple share assumptions and China.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** FY ends late September. Earnings come early Feb, late Apr/early May, late Jul, and early Nov. The Snapdragon Summit (late Sep/Oct) unveils flagship chips. Samsung Galaxy launches (Jan/Feb) and Apple's annual iPhone modem decisions matter, as do investor days and Arm legal developments.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: cheap relative to semis with strong FCF and buybacks; autos, PCs and data center can offset Apple loss; and on-device AI drives premium Android upgrades. Bear: a structurally ex-growth handset business with a known Apple revenue cliff, China concentration, and unproven data-center ambitions; it has been a "value trap" in AI rallies.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.2-1.4, liquid. Earnings-day moves are historically ±3-9%. It tends to range-trade and mean-revert, lagging in AI-led rallies but holding up somewhat better in AI-specific selloffs. Short interest is low. China-trade headlines hit it.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** AAPL, MediaTek (Taiwan-listed), ARM, Skyworks/Qorvo (handset RF), TSM, SOXX. Android OEMs are its customers.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: at the low end of its valuation range with an auto/IoT beat or a credible data-center customer announcement; ahead of a strong Android cycle. Short: into Apple modem transition milestones, China demand weakness or handset destocking; a failed rally to range highs without new catalysts.
- **Verify live:** Apple modem share assumptions, China handset data, data-center customer announcements, Arm litigation status, the latest guide, and tariff/China exposure.

### TXN: Texas Instruments
- **Business:** Analog (power management, signal chain) is roughly three-quarters-plus of revenue, and Embedded Processing (microcontrollers, processors) most of the rest. End markets are dominated by industrial and automotive, with personal electronics, communications equipment and enterprise systems smaller. It is a vertically integrated 300mm manufacturer.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) the analog/industrial cycle: distributor and customer inventory restocking vs. destocking; (2) the capex-heavy 300mm fab buildout (Sherman TX, Lehi UT) and when free cash flow inflects as capex steps down; (3) gross margin, which depends on fab utilization and depreciation; (4) China demand (a significant revenue share) and Chinese domestic analog competition or anti-dumping actions; (5) its dividend and capital-return reputation.
- **Key numbers to check:** next-quarter revenue and EPS guide (midpoint vs. consensus is everything), industrial and auto commentary, gross margin, inventory days (TXN deliberately carries high inventory), capex plan, and FCF.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** calendar FY. Earnings come in roughly the 3rd-4th week of Jan, Apr, Jul and Oct, usually Tuesday after close, often **the first major semi to report**, so it sets the tone for the analog group (ADI, MCHP, NXPI, ON, STM). The capital management call/update (historically around February) also matters.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: a cyclical recovery in industrial and auto meets a lower-cost 300mm footprint, and FCF per share surges as capex falls. Bear: a sluggish recovery, Chinese competition pressures pricing, depreciation weighs on margins, and the multiple already discounts a recovery.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.0-1.2. Its quiet reputation is misleading: earnings-day moves have often been ±5-10% on guidance. It mean-reverts within the cycle, and big trend changes follow inflections in guidance. Short interest is low. In risk-off it behaves as a quality/dividend semi, though still cyclical.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** ADI, MCHP, NXPI, ON, STM, Infineon, SOXX. Auto OEMs and industrial PMIs (ISM manufacturing) are useful macro reads.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: when ISM is turning up, distributors report lean channels, and TXN guides above seasonal. Short: after a recovery rally when guidance comes in at or below seasonal, or China/tariff risk rises; also as a short leg vs. AI semis in a "cyclicals disappoint" tape.
- **Verify live:** the latest guide vs. seasonal, ISM/PMI trend, China tariff/anti-dumping status, capex plan updates, dividend, and the peer read-through from whichever analog name reported first.

### MU: Micron Technology
- **Business:** DRAM is roughly three-quarters of revenue, including **HBM** (high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators), DDR5 and LPDDR. NAND (SSDs) is most of the rest. It reorganized in 2025 into business units around cloud memory, core data center, mobile/client and auto/embedded. Fabs are in the US, Japan, Taiwan and Singapore, with new US fabs under construction.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) DRAM and NAND pricing, the dominant driver since memory is a commodity with oligopoly supply (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron); (2) HBM share, pricing and qualification on NVIDIA/AMD/custom platforms (HBM3E to HBM4); (3) supply discipline: industry capex and wafer starts; (4) data center and AI server demand vs. PC/phone demand; (5) gross margin, which is extremely operating-levered.
- **Key numbers to check:** gross margin guide (the key number), revenue guide, DRAM/NAND bit shipment and pricing commentary, HBM revenue run-rate and share, inventory days, capex, and HBM sold-out/contract commentary for the next year.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** FY ends around late Aug/early Sept. Earnings come late Sep, mid-to-late Dec, late Mar, and late Jun. Read-throughs: SK Hynix and Samsung results and guidance (late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct), TrendForce contract price reports (monthly/quarterly), and NVIDIA platform launches. Export controls on China and Chinese bans on Micron in critical infrastructure (2023) also matter.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: AI makes memory structurally tighter (HBM consumes about three times the wafer area of standard DRAM per bit), making this cycle longer and higher-margin than history. Bear: memory is still a commodity; capex responses (Samsung's HBM catch-up, Chinese CXMT) eventually flood supply, and peak margins mean the stock should sell off on "too good" numbers.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.5-2.0, very liquid. Earnings-day moves are historically ±5-15%. Strongly momentum-driven within cycles, with violent reversals at turns. The stock usually tops before reported margins peak and bottoms while losses are still growing. Short interest is low-moderate.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** SK Hynix, Samsung, SanDisk (SNDK), WDC/STX (storage), NVDA (HBM customer), LRCX/AMAT (memory capex), SMH/SOXX.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: early in a pricing upturn (contract prices inflecting, inventory normalizing), on HBM qualification wins, or after sector selloffs while pricing is still rising. Short: when contract prices plateau, peers announce aggressive capex expansion, or the stock fails to rally on record margins (a classic peak signal).
- **Verify live:** the latest DRAM/NAND contract and spot pricing trend, HBM4 qualification status and share, Samsung/SK Hynix guidance, current cycle position, capex plans, and the implied move.

### AMAT: Applied Materials
- **Business:** Semiconductor Systems (wafer fab equipment: deposition, etch, ion implant, CMP, inspection) is roughly three-quarters of revenue. Applied Global Services (spares, service, upgrades; recurring and steadier) is about a fifth, and Display is small. It is the broadest WFE portfolio in the industry.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) WFE spending outlook, especially leading-edge logic (TSMC, Samsung, Intel) and DRAM/HBM; (2) China, historically a very large revenue share, which is exposed to US export rules (a 2025 BIS rule extending controls to affiliates of listed entities hurt guidance) and to normalization of Chinese domestic purchases; (3) technology inflections: gate-all-around transistors, backside power delivery, advanced packaging and HBM; (4) Intel and Samsung foundry capex cuts.
- **Key numbers to check:** next-quarter revenue/EPS guide, China percentage of revenue, segment mix (foundry/logic vs. DRAM vs. NAND), AGS growth, gross margin, and the company's WFE commentary for the next calendar year.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** FY ends late October. Earnings come mid-Nov, mid-Feb, mid-May, and mid-Aug, typically Thursday after close. TSMC capex guidance (January) and quarterly reports, Samsung/Intel capex announcements and BIS rule updates matter. SEMICON West is in July.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the AI-driven leading-edge and HBM buildout sustains WFE growth, AMAT's share in GAA/backside power rises, and China declines are absorbed. Bear: China normalization plus export rules cut revenue, while non-AI logic/foundry spending (Intel, Samsung) stays weak.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.4-1.7. Earnings-day moves are historically ±4-10%. It trends with the SMH and capex cycle, and is sensitive to export-control headlines (sudden 3-8% drops). Short interest is low.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** LRCX, KLAC, ASML, TEL (Tokyo Electron), TSM (customer), SMH/SOXX. Memory makers (MU, Samsung) are customers.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: when TSMC/memory capex guides rise and China fears are priced in; on dips from broad export-control headlines with limited incremental impact. Short: on new export restrictions, Chinese demand cliff guidance, or WFE outlook cuts by peers; also as a short vs. AI compute when capex shifts toward chips rather than tools.
- **Verify live:** current BIS export rules and the Entity/affiliates list, China revenue share, TSMC/Samsung/Intel capex, WFE year forecast, and the latest guide.

### LRCX: Lam Research
- **Business:** wafer fab equipment focused on **etch and deposition**, the critical steps for 3D NAND layer stacking and high-aspect-ratio DRAM/HBM, plus growing exposure to leading-edge logic (GAA, backside power) and advanced packaging. The Customer Support Business Group (spares, services, upgrades) is a large recurring piece. Historically it is the WFE name most leveraged to memory capex.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) memory capex: NAND technology upgrades (layer-count migrations) and DRAM/HBM buildouts; (2) China exposure, historically a very large revenue share, and export controls; (3) leading-edge logic share gains; (4) WFE year outlook; (5) gross margin from mix and tariff costs.
- **Key numbers to check:** revenue/EPS guide, China percentage of revenue, memory vs. foundry/logic mix, management's WFE outlook, gross margin, and CSBG trends.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** FY ends late June. Earnings come late Jan, late Apr, late Jul, and late Oct, typically Wednesday after close. It usually reports before AMAT and KLAC in each cycle, so it sets the WFE tone. Memory maker capex announcements (Samsung, SK Hynix, MU) and BIS rules also matter. Investor days are held periodically. It did a 10-for-1 split in Oct 2024.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: NAND upgrade spending finally recovers, HBM/DRAM capex surges, and Lam gains share in GAA and advanced packaging. Bear: memory makers stay disciplined, China normalizes downward, and export rules expand.
