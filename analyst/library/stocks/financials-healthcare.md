# Financials & Health Care Dossiers

Reference briefs for 28 large-cap US financials and health care names. Facts are from training knowledge (cutoff around mid-2026). Anything about prices, valuations, guidance or recent quarters is time-sensitive: re-check it under each "Verify live" line before trading.

---

## Primer: what drives these two sectors

#### Financials

**Yield curve and net interest income (NII).** Banks borrow short (deposits) and lend longer, so a steeper curve widens net interest margin (NIM). Higher short rates help at first, but "deposit beta" rises as customers chase yield, as in 2022-23. The market trades **NII guidance** more than headline EPS. Low-yield securities bought in 2020-21 are rolling off, and that fixed-asset repricing is a multi-year NII tailwind for BAC, SCHW and peers.

**Credit cycle.** Under CECL accounting, provisions front-load expected losses, so reserve builds hit EPS before losses show up. Watch card net charge-offs (NCOs), 30+ day delinquencies, office commercial real estate and unemployment. Card lenders (COF) and regionals (KRE) feel it first.

**Capital markets.** M&A, IPO and debt issuance drive investment-banking fees (GS, MS, JPM). Volatility and client activity drive trading. Asset prices drive wealth and asset-management fees (MS, SCHW, BLK). Rising markets plus a deal rebound is the best tape for GS and MS.

**Regulation, stress tests and buybacks.** The Fed's stress test (late June) sets each bank's stress capital buffer (SCB), and dividend and buyback announcements follow. The 2025-26 deregulatory push (a softer Basel III endgame, eSLR relief, stress-test reform) freed excess capital. The market rewards CET1 headroom that turns into buybacks. Consent orders can cap growth, as WFC's asset cap did until 2025.

**Consumer credit.** Card spending and delinquency trends feed V, MA, AXP, COF and PYPL. V and MA carry no credit risk: they are nominal-spending and cross-border-travel plays. Political risks: interchange and routing mandates (Credit Card Competition Act), APR-cap proposals, CFPB actions.

#### Health care

**Patent cliffs.** Loss of exclusivity (LOE) typically removes 50-90% of a small molecule's revenue within a couple of years; biologics erode more slowly. Big LOEs this decade: Keytruda (MRK, 2028), Eliquis (BMS/PFE), Stelara (JNJ, eroding), Prolia (AMGN). The stock trades on whether pipeline and business development fill the hole.

**Pipeline and FDA.** Phase 3 readouts, PDUFA dates (FDA decision deadlines), advisory committees and complete response letters (CRLs, i.e. rejections) are binary events. Data lands at ASCO, ADA, ESMO, AHA and ASH. Large caps move 3-10% on major readouts, single-asset stories far more.

**Drug pricing policy.** IRA Medicare negotiation covers 10 drugs from 2026 and 15 more from 2027, with Part B drugs later. The 2025 most-favored-nation (MFN) push produced company deals (TrumpRx cash prices, Medicaid MFN pricing) in exchange for tariff relief. Headline risk is high, but deals so far mostly triggered "less bad than feared" rallies.

**GLP-1 obesity.** The largest new drug market in decades, led by LLY and Novo Nordisk. Key variables: oral entrants, next-generation assets (amylin, triple agonists, monthly dosing), Medicare coverage, cash-pay pricing. Second-order fears hit bariatric and sleep-apnea devices (ISRG).

**Managed care.** The medical cost ratio (MCR) and cost trend rule. A 2024-25 utilization surge plus Medicare Advantage (MA) risk-adjustment changes (V28) crushed margins. The CMS MA advance notice (late January/early February) and final rate notice (early April) can move UNH and CVS 5-10%. Medicaid cuts, ACA subsidy changes and PBM reform are ongoing risks.

**Tools and biotech funding.** Tools names (TMO, DHR, A) follow biopharma R&D budgets, biotech funding (XBI is the proxy), academic and NIH funding and China. Falling rates help biotech funding and, with a lag, tools demand.

#### Regime behavior

- **Reflation (rising rates, strong growth):** banks and brokers lead (XLF, KRE). Defensive pharma and managed care lag. XBI and long-duration medtech (ISRG) lose on valuation.
- **Easing (falling rates, soft landing):** XBI and tools rally on funding hopes. SCHW and BAC NII is pressured at the margin, but capital markets names gain if growth holds. Payments are steady.
- **Recession or risk-off:** credit names (COF, KRE, C) lead down. Managed care, JNJ, MRK, ABBV, GILD and BRK.B defend, and V and MA dip less than banks. XLV beta is historically ~0.6-0.8.
- **Stagflation:** the hardest regime. Insurers fight cost inflation, banks face credit stress, and pharma with pricing power holds best.
- **Policy shocks** (drug pricing, MA rates, bank rules) hit whole groups and often fade. Separate lasting earnings damage from headline noise.

Proxy ETFs: **XLF** (large financials; BRK.B, JPM, V and MA are its largest holdings), **KRE** (equal-weight regional banks; the purest credit and deposit-stress gauge), **XLV** (large health care; LLY-heavy) and **XBI** (equal-weight small and mid biotech; risk appetite and rates).

## Banks & brokers

### JPM: JPMorgan Chase & Co.
- **Business:** largest US bank. Consumer & Community Banking (Chase retail, cards, mortgage, auto) is roughly 40% of revenue. Commercial & Investment Bank (IB, markets, payments, securities services, commercial banking) is about 45%. Asset & Wealth Management makes up the rest. It is the best-in-class franchise, with returns on tangible common equity (ROTCE) in the high teens to ~20%.
- **What moves the stock:** NII guidance (ex-Markets), markets and IB revenue versus guidance given at mid-quarter conferences, card NCO trend, excess capital and buyback pace, Jamie Dimon commentary on the economy, and CEO succession news.
- **Key numbers to check:** full-year NII and expense guidance, card NCO rate guidance, CET1 versus requirement, IB fee growth, markets revenue year over year, and ROTCE.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports around mid-January, April, July and October, and kicks off bank earnings season. Investor day is typically in May. Mid-quarter conference updates (early March, early June, September, December) often preannounce IB and markets trends. Stress test results come in late June, followed by dividend announcements.
- **Bull case:** the premium franchise compounds book value with fortress capital, and deregulation frees billions for buybacks. **Bear case:** it already trades at a large premium to book, so any credit turn or NII peaking compresses that premium. Dimon warns repeatedly about excess buybacks at high valuations.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.0-1.1. Typical earnings-day move is ±1-4%. Extremely liquid. It trends with the economy and reacts less to idiosyncratic news. In risk-off it falls less than C, KRE or COF. No squeeze risk.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** BAC, WFC, C, XLF. Long JPM / short KRE is a common quality-versus-credit pair.
- **Long setups:** steepening curve, rising IB pipeline, NII guidance raised, pullback to support after a broad bank selloff unrelated to JPM. **Short setups:** extended premium valuation into a guide-down, rising card NCOs, or a recession scare. It is a better short via pairs than outright.
- **Verify live:** latest NII guide, current P/TBV versus history, CET1 ratio, Dimon succession status, card NCO trend.

### BAC: Bank of America
- **Business:** Consumer Banking (the largest deposit franchise), Global Wealth & Investment Management (Merrill, Private Bank), Global Banking and Global Markets. It is the most rate-sensitive of the big four. Its large held-to-maturity securities book, bought in 2020-21 at low yields, is the key swing factor for NII.
- **What moves the stock:** NII trajectory (it guided to NII growth as low-yield securities roll off), deposit growth and cost, long-end rates (the bonds' unrealized losses shrink when yields fall), expense discipline and buybacks. Berkshire Hathaway trimmed its large stake through 2024-25, which created supply overhang headlines.
- **Key numbers to check:** NII and NIM guidance (next-quarter and full-year), average deposits, unrealized losses on the HTM book, consumer NCOs, operating leverage, and wealth net new assets.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports mid-January, April, July and October, usually a day after JPM. Medium-term targets come at investor days, the stress test in late June, and NII commentary at mid-quarter conferences.
- **Bull case:** fixed-rate asset repricing drives multi-year NII growth while efficiency improves, and ROTCE moves toward the mid-to-high teens. **Bear case:** deeper Fed cuts and a flat curve stall NII, and it lacks JPM's fee diversity.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.2-1.3. Typical earnings move ±1-4%. Among the most liquid US stocks. Strongly macro and rates-driven, it tracks the 10-year yield and curve slope. It falls hard in deposit-scare episodes (March 2023).
- **Correlated with / hedges:** JPM, WFC, C, KRE, XLF, and the 2s10s spread.
- **Long setups:** the curve steepens through rising long-end yields with a stable front end, NII guide reaffirmed or raised, sector rotation into value. **Short setups:** bull-flattening (long rates falling on growth fears), deposit outflows, or a failed breakout after earnings.
- **Verify live:** NII guide, Berkshire stake status, deposit trends, unrealized HTM losses, CET1.

### WFC: Wells Fargo
- **Business:** consumer-heavy US bank: Consumer Banking & Lending (branches, cards, auto, mortgage), Commercial Banking, Corporate & Investment Banking, and Wealth & Investment Management. It is building out IB and cards under CEO Charlie Scharf.
- **What moves the stock:** the post-asset-cap story (the Fed lifted the 2018 asset cap in 2025), which allows balance sheet growth and markets expansion. Other drivers are the remaining consent order closures, the efficiency ratio and expense cuts, NII guidance, ROTCE target upgrades and aggressive buybacks.
- **Key numbers to check:** NII guidance, expense guidance, ROTCE versus medium-term target, loan and deposit growth, CET1, and buyback amount.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports mid-month in January, April, July and October, the same day as JPM. Regulatory actions (consent order terminations) can come anytime. Stress test in late June.
- **Bull case:** freed from the cap, it re-levers its balance sheet, grows fee businesses and moves ROTCE into the high teens with heavy buybacks. **Bear case:** the turnaround is priced in, NII is pressured by rate cuts, and investment spending lifts expenses.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.1-1.3. Earnings-day moves of ±2-6% are common. It has had sharp negative reactions to NII guide-downs. Very liquid. Momentum-friendly when the turnaround narrative is working.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** BAC, JPM, USB, PNC, KRE, XLF.
- **Long setups:** regulatory milestone news, ROTCE target raised, a dip on a sector-wide selloff. **Short setups:** NII guide-down plus higher expense guidance, or a fading turnaround narrative after a big run.
- **Verify live:** remaining consent orders, current ROTCE target, NII guide, any leadership changes.

### GS: Goldman Sachs
- **Business:** Global Banking & Markets (IB advisory and underwriting, FICC and equities trading, including prime brokerage and financing) is roughly 70% of revenue. Asset & Wealth Management (management fees, private wealth, alternatives, principal investments) makes up most of the rest. It has been retreating from consumer banking (Marcus, GM card, Apple Card wind-down).
- **What moves the stock:** the M&A and IPO cycle (it is #1 in announced M&A), equities trading (prime balances, financing revenue), alternatives fundraising and AWM fee growth, and lumpy principal investment marks. It is a high-beta proxy for "animal spirits."
- **Key numbers to check:** IB fees and backlog commentary, equities and FICC revenue versus peers, AWM pre-tax margin, alternatives fundraising, ROE versus the mid-teens target, and CET1 headroom.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports mid-month in January, April, July and October (bank week). Conference commentary arrives mid-quarter. Big deal announcements and IPO windows reopening are tells. Stress test SCB in late June.
- **Bull case:** a multi-year M&A and IPO upcycle under lighter regulation, plus durable fee growth in wealth and alternatives, re-rates it toward MS. **Bear case:** capital markets are cyclical, so a risk-off shock freezes deals and it de-rates quickly. Principal investment losses are possible.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.3-1.5. Earnings moves of ±2-6%. Very liquid with a high share price. Trends strongly with SPX and deal sentiment, and falls hard in risk-off (VIX spikes).
- **Correlated with / hedges:** MS, JPM, the IB boutiques (EVR, LAZ, PJT), SPX. Long GS / short XLF isolates capital markets.
- **Long setups:** M&A announcements accelerating, IPO calendar reopening, equities volatility with high client activity, strong peer IB commentary. **Short setups:** a deal freeze (tariff or geopolitical shock), credit spread widening, a disappointing backlog.
- **Verify live:** IB pipeline commentary, current ROE, consumer exit status, valuation versus MS.

### MS: Morgan Stanley
- **Business:** Wealth Management (including E*TRADE, workplace and advisor channels) is roughly half of revenue and the core of the valuation. Institutional Securities (IB and trading) is roughly 40-45%. Investment Management (including Eaton Vance and Parametric) is the rest.
- **What moves the stock:** net new assets (NNA) in wealth, the wealth pre-tax margin (target 30%), market levels (fee-based assets), equities trading strength (a top-tier prime broker), the IB recovery, and the capital return and dividend story.
- **Key numbers to check:** wealth NNA (it targets about $1T in new assets over multiple years), fee-based flows, the wealth margin, firm ROTCE versus its ~20% target, equities trading revenue, and the efficiency ratio.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports mid-month in January, April, July and October (usually a day after JPM, with BAC). It sometimes publishes monthly or interim metrics. Stress test SCB in late June. Leadership is CEO Ted Pick.
- **Bull case:** a wealth-led "annuity" earnings stream plus a cyclical IB rebound justify a premium multiple and steady buybacks. **Bear case:** an equity market drawdown hits fee-based assets and trading together, and NNA slowing hurts the narrative.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.2-1.4. Earnings moves of ±2-5%. Very liquid. Highly correlated to SPX levels and strongly trend-following in bull markets.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** GS, SCHW, RJF, LPLA, SPX.
- **Long setups:** rising equity markets with strong NNA, an IB recovery, a breakout after earnings. **Short setups:** a sharp equity correction, an NNA miss, or a margin target slipping.
- **Verify live:** latest NNA, wealth margin, ROTCE guidance, valuation premium versus GS.

### C: Citigroup
- **Business:** a global bank under restructuring with five segments: Services (Treasury & Trade Solutions, Securities Services), Markets, Banking, US Personal Banking (branded cards, retail services, i.e. private label) and Wealth. It is exiting international consumer markets, including the separation of Banamex in Mexico through a stake sale and planned IPO.
- **What moves the stock:** the credibility of CEO Jane Fraser's transformation, the ROTCE target (10-11% for 2026), expense cuts, progress on regulatory consent orders (data and risk management), buybacks (a large program while it trades near or below tangible book), Banamex milestones, and US card credit.
- **Key numbers to check:** ROTCE versus target, the expense guide, Services revenue growth, card NCOs (Branded and Retail Services), CET1 versus requirement, the buyback pace, and P/TBV.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports mid-month in January, April, July and October. Investor day is a big event. Watch for regulatory penalties or consent order releases, Banamex IPO timing, and the late-June stress test.
- **Bull case:** a classic re-rating from a discount to tangible book toward or above it as ROTCE rises, with heavy buybacks shrinking the share count. **Bear case:** another regulatory setback or rising expenses, plus EM or global macro shocks. The discount has been persistent for years.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.3-1.5, the highest of the big four. Earnings moves of ±2-6%. Very liquid. Momentum works during re-rating phases, but it falls hardest among megabanks in risk-off.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** BAC, JPM, WFC, XLF, and EM FX and EM equities to some degree.
- **Long setups:** on-track restructuring milestones, ROTCE beats, buyback acceleration, value rotation. **Short setups:** regulatory penalty headlines, a missed expense target, EM stress, a global recession scare.
- **Verify live:** current P/TBV, ROTCE target status, consent order status, Banamex timeline.

### SCHW: Charles Schwab
- **Business:** the largest retail brokerage and RIA custodian. It earns net interest revenue on client cash swept to its bank (roughly half of revenue in normal times), asset management and administration fees, and trading revenue. TD Ameritrade integration is complete.
- **What moves the stock:** "cash sorting" (clients moving sweep cash into higher-yielding money funds, which crushed NII in 2023), NIM recovery as high-cost bank supplemental funding is paid down, core net new assets, trading activity (DATs, daily average trades), and the buyback restart.
- **Key numbers to check:** monthly activity reports (released mid-month: core NNA, new accounts, client cash, margin balances), NIM guidance, bank supplemental funding balances, and the expense guide.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** quarterly reports in mid-January, April, July and October. The monthly metrics are real catalysts. Fed rate path and money-fund yield changes also matter. A Winter Business Update typically comes early in the year.
- **Bull case:** cash sorting is over, NIM expands as expensive funding rolls off, organic growth runs around 5-7% per year, and record trading activity drives operating leverage plus big buybacks. **Bear case:** rapid rate cuts compress the cash yield, retail trading slows in a bear market, and it remains a rate-and-market double play.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.0-1.3. Earnings moves of ±2-6%. It moved violently in March 2023 (deposit fears). Liquid. Tracks retail activity, SPX and rate expectations. It can squeeze after over-pessimism.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** IBKR, HOOD, MS, LPLA, KRE (in stress episodes).
- **Long setups:** strong monthly NNA plus rising margin balances, active retail markets, NIM guide up. **Short setups:** a surge in cash sorting, sharp rate cuts, retail volume collapse.
- **Verify live:** latest monthly metrics, NIM guidance, supplemental funding level, buyback status.

## Payments & consumer finance

### V: Visa
- **Business:** the global card network. It earns service revenue (on payment volume), data processing revenue (on processed transactions), international transaction revenue (cross-border, the highest margin) and value-added services, minus client incentives. It takes no credit risk.
- **What moves the stock:** US payments volume growth (nominal consumer spending), cross-border volume growth (travel and e-commerce), net revenue growth versus its low-double-digit algorithm, regulatory and legal risk (DOJ debit antitrust suit, the Credit Card Competition Act, merchant interchange litigation), and disruption narratives (stablecoins, account-to-account payments, which it is co-opting).
- **Key numbers to check:** US payments volume growth, cross-border volume ex-intra-Europe, processed transactions, net revenue growth guidance, client incentives as a share of gross revenue, and the intra-quarter volume update given on the call.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** the fiscal year ends in September. It reports late January, April, July and October. Intra-quarter spending trends come on the call. Watch legislative headlines (CCCA amendments), DOJ case milestones and interchange settlement court rulings.
- **Bull case:** a compounding toll road with low-teens EPS growth, massive buybacks and new flows (B2B, value-added services). **Bear case:** regulation finally bites (routing mandates, interchange caps), or the stablecoin/fintech threat compresses the multiple.
- **Trading character:** beta around 0.9-1.0. Earnings moves ±1-4%. Very liquid. A "quality compounder" that grinds upward and mean-reverts on regulatory headlines. Defensive within financials during credit scares.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** MA (very high correlation), AXP, PYPL. Long V / short MA is low-volatility, so trade relative cross-border exposure.
- **Long setups:** pullbacks on regulatory headlines that don't change earnings, cross-border acceleration (travel strength), rotation to quality. **Short setups:** a credible legislative push on CCCA, a sharp consumer slowdown, a stablecoin narrative spike. Better as a pairs short.
- **Verify live:** DOJ case status, CCCA status, latest volume trends, interchange settlement status.

### MA: Mastercard
- **Business:** the #2 global card network: payment network revenue plus value-added services & solutions (cyber, data analytics, loyalty, open banking), which grow faster. It is more international and more cross-border-heavy than Visa, with a calendar fiscal year.
- **What moves the stock:** cross-border volume (travel), gross dollar volume growth, VAS growth (a mid-to-high-teens contributor), net revenue growth versus its low-teens algorithm, and the same regulatory and legal overhangs as Visa.
- **Key numbers to check:** net revenue growth (currency-neutral), cross-border volume growth, switched transactions, VAS growth, rebates and incentives, and the operating expense guide.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports late January, late April/early May, late July and late October. Investor day is periodic. The same regulatory items apply as for V.
- **Bull case:** a slightly faster grower than V thanks to cross-border and VAS. Compounding plus buybacks deliver mid-teens EPS growth. **Bear case:** a travel slowdown or FX volatility hits cross-border, and regulatory risk persists.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.0-1.1. Earnings moves ±1-4%. Very liquid. Moves almost in lockstep with V and tends to trend smoothly.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** V, AXP, PYPL, FI, and travel names (BKNG) via cross-border.
- **Long setups:** strong travel data, a selloff on non-fundamental regulatory noise, a breakout after earnings. **Short setups:** travel or cross-border deceleration, an FX shock, a growth scare.
- **Verify live:** cross-border trends, any legal settlements, valuation premium versus V.

### AXP: American Express
- **Business:** a closed-loop network plus card issuer. Revenue is mostly discount revenue (merchant fees on billed business), with growing net card fees and net interest income on revolving loans. Its customers skew affluent, and it has been growing Millennial and Gen-Z card members.
- **What moves the stock:** billed business growth (especially T&E, i.e. travel and entertainment), card fee growth (Platinum refresh cycles), NII from loan growth, credit quality (historically the best among card issuers), and EPS guidance (a mid-teens growth aspiration).
- **Key numbers to check:** billed business growth (FX-adjusted), net card fee growth, new card acquisitions, write-off and delinquency rates, the revenue growth guide (low double digits) and the EPS guide, plus marketing expense.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports in the third or fourth week of January, April, July and October (often on a Friday). Card refresh announcements matter (the Platinum fee rose substantially in 2025 with added benefits). Stress test in late June. Watch political card-rate or fee proposals.
- **Bull case:** affluent spending is resilient, fee-based revenue is sticky, and it compounds EPS mid-teens with buybacks. **Bear case:** a premium valuation versus banks, plus a T&E slowdown or rising marketing and reward costs that squeeze margins.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.1-1.3. Earnings moves ±2-6%. Liquid. Trends well with consumer confidence and travel and drops in recession scares. A long-time Berkshire holding.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** COF, V, MA, SYF, and travel stocks.
- **Long setups:** strong spending prints and card-fee momentum, a pullback in an uptrend, an affluent-consumer strength narrative. **Short setups:** a T&E slowdown, rising delinquencies, a guide-down on expenses.
- **Verify live:** latest billed business trend, guidance, credit metrics, any APR-cap or regulatory proposals.

### PYPL: PayPal Holdings
- **Business:** digital payments. It runs branded checkout (PayPal button, Pay Later), Braintree (unbranded processing for large merchants: high volume, low margin), Venmo (P2P, now monetizing via debit card and Pay with Venmo) and Xoom. Revenue comes from transaction take rates.
- **What moves the stock:** branded checkout TPV growth (the most important KPI; it has been sluggish against Apple Pay and Shop Pay), transaction margin dollars (TM$) growth, Braintree repricing, Venmo monetization, the turnaround under CEO Alex Chriss, cost cuts and big buybacks.
- **Key numbers to check:** TM$ growth (ex-interest on customer balances), branded checkout growth, total payment volume (TPV), active accounts and transactions per active, non-GAAP EPS guidance, and free cash flow.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports early February, late April/early May, late July/early August and late October/early November. Product launches, Investor Day, partnership deals and agentic-commerce news also move it.
- **Bull case:** cheap on a P/E and FCF basis with a large buyback. If branded checkout re-accelerates, the multiple re-rates. **Bear case:** a structural share loss in checkout to Apple Pay and Shop Pay makes it a value trap where every quarter disappoints on branded growth.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.2-1.5. Earnings-day moves are big, often ±7-15%. Liquid. Heavily sentiment-driven, with sharp gap moves both ways. It can squeeze on decent prints given skeptical positioning.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** XYZ (Block), AFRM, SHOP (as a competitor), V and MA (loosely).
- **Long setups:** branded checkout growth inflecting up, oversold into earnings with low expectations, a large buyback authorization. **Short setups:** a guidance cut, more checkout deceleration, a bounce into resistance after a failed turnaround catalyst.
- **Verify live:** latest branded checkout growth, guidance, CEO and strategy updates, short interest.

### COF: Capital One Financial
- **Business:** a big card lender (domestic card is the largest profit driver), auto lending, consumer banking and commercial banking. It acquired Discover Financial (closed in 2025), which gives it its own payment network. It is moving debit and some credit volume onto Discover and Pulse.
- **What moves the stock:** consumer credit (card NCOs and delinquencies), Discover integration synergies (cost plus network savings), the network migration narrative ("the next V/MA-like asset"), buyback capacity, and its high NIM.
- **Key numbers to check:** the **monthly credit card master trust data** (8-K mid-month with NCO and delinquency rates), quarterly NCO and provision, the reserve coverage ratio, NIM, the efficiency ratio, integration cost guidance, and CET1.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports in the third or fourth week of January, April, July and October (after the close). The monthly credit data is a real catalyst. Integration milestones, the late-June stress test, and political APR or late-fee rules matter.
- **Bull case:** Discover synergies plus network economics drive outsized EPS growth while credit stays benign, with big buybacks from excess capital. **Bear case:** an unemployment uptick drives a sharp rise in card NCOs, integration hiccups, regulatory headlines on card rates.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.4-1.6. Earnings moves ±3-7%. Liquid. Highly sensitive to unemployment data and consumer credit headlines. Falls sharply in recession scares and rebounds fast.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** SYF, AXP, DFS (historical), ALLY, KRE. Short COF / long V is a credit-versus-network pair.
- **Long setups:** improving or stable monthly delinquency data, synergy upside, a dip from macro fears without credit deterioration. **Short setups:** rising jobless claims plus worsening monthly delinquencies, card-rate cap headlines, integration setbacks.
- **Verify live:** monthly trust data, NCO guide, integration status, CET1 and buyback, political risk on card APRs.

## Asset managers & conglomerates

### BLK: BlackRock
- **Business:** the world's largest asset manager. iShares ETFs provide the bulk of flows, alongside index institutional, active, cash and private markets (expanded via the GIP infrastructure, HPS private credit and Preqin data deals). Technology services (Aladdin) provide recurring revenue. Base fees are tied to AUM, plus performance fees.
- **What moves the stock:** global equity and bond market levels (which drive AUM), organic base fee growth (its target is 5%+), private markets fundraising and fee-rate mix, Aladdin ACV growth, and operating margin.
- **Key numbers to check:** total net inflows, organic base fee growth, AUM, the adjusted operating margin, technology services ACV (annual contract value) growth, and performance fees.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports mid-January, April, July and October, often on the first day of bank earnings. Investor day. Monthly AUM data and ETF flow trackers.
- **Bull case:** dominant ETF scale plus a private markets build-out raise the blended fee rate and growth, and it deserves a premium multiple. **Bear case:** market drawdowns cut AUM, fee compression in ETFs, and integration and execution risk in privates.
- **Trading character:** beta around 1.1-1.3. Earnings moves ±2-5%. Liquid but with a high share price. Behaves like a levered SPX proxy.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** SPX, KKR, BX, APO (private markets), MS, SCHW.
- **Long setups:** rising markets plus organic growth above target, strong private markets fundraising news. **Short setups:** a market correction, an organic growth miss, margin pressure.
- **Verify live:** latest AUM and flows, organic growth rate, private markets fundraising, valuation.

### BRK.B: Berkshire Hathaway (Class B)
- **Business:** a conglomerate. Insurance (GEICO, General Re, Berkshire Hathaway Reinsurance and Primary) provides float and underwriting income. BNSF railroad, Berkshire Hathaway Energy, manufacturing, service and retail businesses (Precision Castparts, Lubrizol, Pilot, etc.) make up the rest. It holds a large equity portfolio (Apple, AXP, BAC, KO, CVX, OXY, Japanese trading houses) and an enormous cash and Treasury bill pile. Greg Abel became CEO at the start of 2026, and Warren Buffett remains chairman.
- **What moves the stock:** buybacks (which signal management sees value), insurance underwriting cycles (GEICO margins, catastrophe losses), operating earnings trends (rail volumes, utility liabilities such as wildfire exposure), how the cash pile is deployed (large acquisitions), and post-Buffett governance and capital allocation perception.
- **Key numbers to check:** operating earnings (ignore GAAP investment gains/losses), insurance underwriting profit, float, the cash and T-bill balance, the buyback amount (often zero recently), the 13F equity portfolio changes, and BNSF operating ratio.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** reports on a Saturday morning around the first weekend of May, August and November, plus late February with the annual letter. The annual meeting is in early May. 13F filings come mid-February, May, August and November. Large deals are announced anytime.
- **Bull case:** a fortress balance sheet with optionality to deploy a huge cash pile in a downturn, a diversified earnings stream, and a low-beta compounder. **Bear case:** a post-Buffett "key-man" discount, too much cash dragging returns when rates fall, and a lack of buybacks signaling that management considers the stock expensive.
