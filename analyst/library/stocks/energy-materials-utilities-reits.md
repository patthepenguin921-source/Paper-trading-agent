# Energy, Materials & Metals, Utilities, REITs: Dossiers

Coverage: XOM, CVX, COP, SLB, OXY (Energy) · LIN, FCX, NEM (Materials & metals) · NEE, DUK, SO (Utilities) · AMT, PLD (REITs).
Proxy ETFs: XLE (energy), XLB (materials), XLU (utilities), XLRE (real estate), GLD (gold), USO (front-end WTI futures).

> This file was written from background knowledge with a cutoff. Prices, yields, production guidance, deal status and management names change. Anything time-sensitive is under **Verify live**. Treat ranges as rough historical tendencies, not current facts.

## Primer: what actually drives these sectors

**Oil and gas.** Crude is a global commodity priced at the margin by supply discipline and inventory. The big supply lever is **OPEC+** (Saudi Arabia plus Russia and allies). Meeting outcomes, voluntary cuts being unwound or extended, and compliance by over-producers move Brent and WTI. US shale is the swing non-OPEC producer. Watch the **Baker Hughes rig count** (Fridays) and frac-spread counts as lagging indicators of supply response, and listen to E&P capex guidance. Shale now favors returning cash over growth, so supply responds slowly to price. **Inventories** are the scoreboard: the EIA weekly petroleum status report (Wednesdays, with API data Tuesday evening) shows crude, gasoline and distillate builds and draws and Cushing levels. Draws against seasonal norms are bullish. On the **demand** side, the drivers are China (imports, refinery runs, stimulus), global PMIs, jet fuel and US driving season. Recession fears hit oil hard. **Geopolitics** (Middle East, Russia sanctions, Red Sea shipping, Venezuela and Iran sanctions) adds a risk premium that tends to fade fast unless barrels are actually lost. **Crack spreads** (the 3-2-1 spread: gasoline plus diesel minus crude) set refining margins, which matter for the integrated majors' downstream segments. Natural gas (Henry Hub) is regional and driven by weather, storage (EIA report on Thursdays) and LNG export capacity. The LNG build-out on the US Gulf Coast is a structural tailwind for gas demand.

**Sub-industry behavior.** *Integrated majors* (XOM, CVX) combine upstream, refining and chemicals, have fortress balance sheets, pay dividends and buy back stock. They have the lowest beta to crude and are often owned as defensive value. *E&Ps* (COP, OXY) are pure upstream with the highest torque to oil, especially when leveraged. *Services* (SLB) lag the commodity because operators decide activity after prices move, so services are levered to the *capex cycle* (international and offshore matters more than US land now). Energy equities usually track the oil price and the *back end* of the futures curve more than spot. A spike in the front month that leaves 12-to-24-month futures unchanged moves the stocks much less than the commodity. Energy is a classic inflation hedge, and it rallies when the market rotates from growth into value.

**Copper and gold.** Copper is the industrial bellwether. China consumes roughly half of world supply, so property starts, the grid and stimulus matter. The supply story is tight: few large new mines, falling ore grades, and disruptions in Chile, Peru, the DRC and Indonesia. Electrification, EVs, grid spending and data centers are the structural demand narrative. Tariff policy (US Section 232 actions on copper) has caused COMEX/LME spread dislocations. Gold is a monetary asset. It historically trades inversely to **real rates** (TIPS yields) and the **US dollar**, but since 2022 heavy **central-bank buying** (China, Poland, Turkey, India and others diversifying away from dollar reserves) has partly broken the real-rate link. ETF flows, geopolitical fear and fiscal-debasement worries add bids. Miners are operationally levered to the metal: when all-in sustaining cost (AISC) is fixed, margins expand faster than price rises, but cost inflation and execution problems hurt.

**Utilities and REITs as bond proxies.** Both pay high, steady dividends and fund themselves with debt, so they trade inversely to long-term Treasury yields (10Y and 30Y). Falling yields mean they outperform; rising yields mean they underperform. Utilities earn a regulated return on rate base, so growth = rate-base growth (capex) times the allowed ROE, and regulatory outcomes are key. REITs are valued on FFO/AFFO multiples and cap rates. Higher rates raise cap rates and borrowing costs. **The AI/data-center power theme** has re-rated utilities since 2024. Load-growth forecasts that were flat for 15 years now point up because of hyperscaler data centers, onshoring and electrification. Utilities with large interconnection queues (Southeast, Texas, PJM), nuclear fleets, or gas-generation build-outs have traded partly as AI derivatives. That adds momentum and also makes them vulnerable to AI-capex scares (for example, the DeepSeek drawdown in January 2025). For REITs, data-center, tower and logistics landlords carry secular themes on top of rate sensitivity.

**Quick ratio heuristics.** XLE vs SPY shows inflation/value rotation. XLU vs SPY shows defensiveness, unless the AI-power bid is driving it. XLRE vs TLT shows whether REITs are just tracking rates. Copper/gold rising signals growth optimism and usually higher yields.

## Energy

### XOM: Exxon Mobil
- **Business:** Largest US integrated oil major. Upstream is anchored by the Permian (scale greatly increased by the Pioneer Natural Resources acquisition, closed 2024) and Guyana's Stabroek block (operator, roughly 45%), plus LNG. It also has large Energy Products (refining), Chemical Products and Specialty Products segments, and a Low Carbon Solutions unit (CCS, hydrogen, lithium).
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Brent/WTI and the back end of the curve. (2) Refining margins and chemical margins (chemicals are weak when China oversupplies). (3) Guyana and Permian volume growth and execution on cost-savings targets. (4) Buyback pace and dividend growth. (5) Rotation flows: it is the largest XLE weight, so passive and value flows hit it.
- **Key numbers to check:** Upstream production (koebd), Permian and Guyana volumes, earnings by segment, cash flow from operations vs capex plus dividends, buyback guidance, net debt/capital, structural cost savings vs target.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Quarterly earnings late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, typically a Friday, often the same day as CVX. It pre-releases "earnings considerations" (an 8-K) early in the month after quarter-end, showing the sensitivity of earnings to price and margin changes; read it. The annual Corporate Plan update is in December. The dividend increase has historically been announced in late Q4. Watch OPEC+ meetings and new Guyana FPSO start-ups.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: low-cost advantaged barrels (Guyana, Permian) grow volumes at high returns, buybacks shrink the share count, and it is the best-in-class balance sheet. Bear: oil downcycle, prolonged chemical-margin trough, Guyana border risk with Venezuela, and capital intensity of growth projects in a falling-price tape.
- **Trading character:** Beta to SPY well below 1. Beta to oil is meaningful but damped. Earnings-day moves historically about ±1-4%. It trends with oil over weeks and mean-reverts intraday. In broad risk-off it often holds up better than the market unless oil is also falling (recession scare).
- **Correlated with / hedges:** CVX (very high correlation), XLE, Brent. Pair: long XOM / short CVX (or the reverse) isolates company execution. Hedge sector beta with XLE or USO.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: oil is basing with inventory draws and OPEC+ discipline, and XOM pulls back to support while the buyback runs. Short: oil is rolling over on a demand scare, chemical margins are compressing, or it has rallied on a geopolitical spike that is likely to fade.
- **Verify live:** Current Brent/WTI and curve shape, latest production guidance, buyback run-rate, dividend yield, next earnings date, Guyana project status.

### CVX: Chevron
- **Business:** Second-largest US integrated major. Upstream assets in the Permian, DJ Basin, Gulf of America (Gulf of Mexico), Tengiz in Kazakhstan (via Tengizchevroil), Australian LNG (Gorgon/Wheatstone), and a 30% Stabroek (Guyana) stake via the Hess acquisition, completed in 2025 after arbitration with XOM over the pre-emption right. It has a downstream/chemicals segment (CPChem JV with Phillips 66). Headquarters moved to Houston.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Oil price. (2) Execution on the TCO expansion (FGP/WPMP) ramp and Hess integration synergies. (3) Buyback size (historically flexed with oil) and dividend growth. (4) Venezuela license and sanctions headlines. (5) Cost-reduction program delivery.
- **Key numbers to check:** Production growth guidance, Permian volumes, TCO free cash flow distributions, free cash flow breakeven, buyback guidance range, net debt ratio, downstream margins.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct (often the same Friday as XOM). Investor day or guidance update usually around Q4/Q1. Dividend raise historically in late January. Kazakhstan and Venezuela policy headlines.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: the Hess/Guyana and TCO step-up drives multi-year FCF growth, and a high yield plus buybacks make it a cash machine. Bear: higher-cost portfolio than XOM, project execution history (TCO overruns), geopolitical concentration (Kazakhstan, Venezuela), and a dividend that is stretched if oil falls hard.
- **Trading character:** Beta below 1. Earnings moves historically about ±1-4%. Tracks XOM closely but has more idiosyncratic risk around buyback cuts. Defensive in inflationary risk-off; weak in recessionary risk-off.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** XOM, XLE, COP. Hedge with XLE or a short in XOM for a relative-value view.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: the yield gap vs XOM is wide while catalysts (TCO ramp, Guyana volumes) are ahead, and oil is stable. Short: a buyback cut or guidance miss, or oil breaking down with CVX underperforming XOM on execution worries.
- **Verify live:** Hess integration progress, TCO ramp status, current buyback range, Venezuela license status, dividend yield, next earnings date.

### COP: ConocoPhillips
- **Business:** Largest US independent E&P (no refining). Lower 48 (Permian, Eagle Ford, Bakken), Alaska (including the Willow project under development), Canada (Montney, oil sands), Norway, and Qatar/LNG interests (including offtake from Port Arthur LNG). Scale increased by the Marathon Oil acquisition (closed late 2024).
- **What moves the stock:** (1) WTI price; higher torque than the majors. (2) Capital-return framework (it targets returning a large share of CFO). (3) Capex discipline and Willow and LNG project costs. (4) Synergy capture from Marathon. (5) Natural gas and LNG price exposure, secondarily.
- **Key numbers to check:** Production (MBOED) vs guidance, capex guidance, return of capital (buybacks plus dividends), free cash flow at different oil prices, Lower 48 well productivity, Willow capex and timing.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings early Feb/May/Aug and late Oct/early Nov (typically a Thursday). Periodic analyst/investor meetings with multi-year plans. OPEC+ and EIA weekly data.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: deep, low-cost inventory, long-dated growth (Willow, LNG) and steady capital returns make it the "quality E&P." Bear: purest oil beta among the large caps, long-cycle project costs during a downturn, and exposure to US policy on Alaska permitting.
- **Trading character:** Beta to oil higher than XOM/CVX. Beta to SPY around 1. Earnings moves historically about ±2-5%. Stronger momentum around oil trend breaks. In recessionary risk-off it falls harder than the majors.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** Other E&Ps (EOG, DVN, FANG), OXY, USO. Long COP / short XLE expresses "E&P outperforms." Hedge with USO or crude futures exposure.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: oil curve steepening up, positive inventory surprises, COP lagging its peers without a fundamental cause. Short: OPEC+ adds supply into weak demand, front-month crude breaks support, and COP rolls over first given its higher beta.
- **Verify live:** Production and capex guidance, Willow schedule and cost, capital-return target, current WTI, next earnings date.

### SLB: SLB (formerly Schlumberger)
- **Business:** Largest oilfield services company. Reservoir characterization, well construction (drilling), production systems, and Digital (software, cloud, AI). Revenue is weighted to international and offshore markets (Middle East, especially Saudi Arabia, plus Latin America and offshore), much less to US land than peers like HAL. It acquired ChampionX (production chemicals; closed 2025) and has promoted data-center-related infrastructure solutions as a growth line.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) International/offshore upstream capex cycle. (2) Saudi Aramco and Middle East activity levels (Saudi slowdowns hurt). (3) Oil price with a lag; operators cut budgets after price drops. (4) Digital revenue growth and margins. (5) Pricing power vs rig count trends.
- **Key numbers to check:** Revenue by division and by geography (international vs North America), pre-tax segment margins, Digital growth, free cash flow, shareholder returns, book-to-bill and backlog commentary, and peer commentary from HAL and BKR.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Usually first major energy reporter: earnings mid-to-late Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, often a Friday, which sets the tone for the services group. Rig counts on Fridays. Aramco capex announcements. OPEC+ meetings.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: multi-year international/offshore up-cycle, technology leadership, margin expansion from digital, capital-light returns. Bear: international spending plateau (Saudi pullback, Mexico payment issues), oil below the level that sustains budgets, and services pricing deflation.
- **Trading character:** Higher beta than the integrateds; trades like a cyclical. Earnings moves historically about ±2-6%. Momentum-driven around guidance and capex cycles. Underperforms in risk-off and oil sell-offs.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** HAL, BKR, XLE, OIH. Long SLB / short HAL expresses an international-over-US-land view. Hedge with XLE or OIH.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: an oil recovery is seen as durable, Middle East/offshore contract wins, and the stock lags while the earnings revision trend turns up. Short: management flags softening activity, oil falls toward levels that trigger budget cuts, or a big Saudi pullback.
- **Verify live:** Latest guidance on international growth, Saudi activity commentary, ChampionX integration, buyback plan, next earnings date.

### OXY: Occidental Petroleum
- **Business:** Large US E&P, Permian-heavy (expanded by the CrownRock acquisition, 2024), plus Rockies, Gulf of America, Oman, Algeria and UAE. Midstream assets including a stake in Western Midstream. Low Carbon Ventures: direct air capture (1PointFive, the Stratos project in Texas) and CO2 enhanced oil recovery. It agreed in 2025 to sell its OxyChem chemicals business to Berkshire Hathaway to accelerate debt reduction (verify closing). Berkshire is the largest shareholder (roughly a quarter of common stock historically) and also holds preferred shares and warrants.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) WTI. Leveraged balance sheet means high equity torque. (2) Deleveraging progress (debt reduction targets, asset sales). (3) Berkshire buying or holding news (sentiment floor). (4) Preferred redemption mechanics (common buybacks can trigger preferred redemption obligations). (5) DAC/carbon-capture policy (45Q credits).
- **Key numbers to check:** Net debt and principal-reduction progress, production vs guidance, Permian well costs, free cash flow after dividends, preferred balance, Berkshire stake (13F/Form 4).
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings early-to-mid Feb, May, Aug, Nov (typically after the close). Berkshire 13F filings (mid-Feb/May/Aug/Nov). Asset-sale announcements. 45Q policy changes.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: deleveraging shifts value from debt to equity, quality Permian inventory, Berkshire backstop, a free option on carbon capture. Bear: highest leverage among the large caps, so it underperforms most in an oil downturn; DAC is cash-hungry with uncertain returns; perceived Berkshire floor can make longs complacent.
- **Trading character:** Beta to oil is the highest in this group. Beta to SPY above 1 in oil-driven tapes. Earnings moves historically about ±2-6%. Strongly trend-following with oil. In risk-off it sells off hard with crude.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** COP, Permian E&Ps (FANG, EOG), USO, XOP. Long OXY / short COP is a levered-beta oil bet. Hedge with USO or XLE.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: oil is turning up from a washout and deleveraging milestones are hit (asset sale closes). Short: oil breaks down, debt targets slip, or the stock rallies on Berkshire headlines without fundamental change.
- **Verify live:** OxyChem sale status, net debt, Berkshire stake size, production guidance, WTI, next earnings date.

## Materials & metals

### LIN: Linde plc
- **Business:** World's largest industrial gases company (oxygen, nitrogen, argon, hydrogen, CO2, helium) after the Praxair/Linde merger (2018). Revenue comes from on-site supply under long-term take-or-pay contracts, merchant (bulk) and packaged gases, and an engineering business that builds plants. End markets: healthcare, chemicals and energy, manufacturing, metals and mining, food and beverage, and electronics (semiconductor fabs). Clean hydrogen and decarbonization projects make up part of the backlog.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Pricing power. It reliably pushes price above inflation. (2) Volumes tied to global industrial production (weak in Europe and China slowdowns). (3) Project backlog (sale-of-gas backlog), especially electronics and clean energy. (4) EPS growth consistency and buybacks. (5) Rotation into defensive quality/compounders.
- **Key numbers to check:** EPS growth (ex-FX) vs guidance, price vs volume contribution, operating margin, ROC, project backlog, FX impact, capex and buybacks.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings late Apr/early May, late Jul/early Aug, late Oct/early Nov, and early Feb (often before the open). Guidance is typically given as a full-year EPS range with an FX assumption. Watch global PMIs.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: oligopoly with contractual floors, steady high-single to low-double-digit EPS growth, margin expansion through pricing, and secular wins in electronics and energy transition. Bear: premium multiple vulnerable to de-rating, weak industrial volumes, clean-hydrogen projects delayed or uneconomic, FX headwinds from a strong dollar.
- **Trading character:** Low beta (below 1). Earnings moves historically small (about ±1-4%). Steady trend follower; deep drawdowns are rare and tend to get bought. In risk-off it acts more defensive than most materials names.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** APD (peer, more volatile), AI.PA (Air Liquide), XLB (largest weight), and quality-factor ETFs. Pair: long LIN / short APD has historically favored LIN's execution. Hedge with XLB.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: pullback to the long-term trend in a soft macro tape while pricing holds, or a post-earnings dip on FX noise only. Short: rarely a good standalone short; consider it only on a multiple de-rating with price/volume deterioration, or as the short leg against cyclical materials in a recovery.
- **Verify live:** EPS guidance, backlog size, volume trends, FX assumptions, valuation vs history, next earnings date.

### FCX: Freeport-McMoRan
- **Business:** One of the largest publicly traded copper producers, with significant gold and molybdenum. Key assets: the Grasberg minerals district in Indonesia (via PT Freeport Indonesia, where Indonesia's government owns a majority stake), North America (Morenci and other Arizona operations), and South America (Cerro Verde in Peru). A mud-rush incident at the Grasberg Block Cave mine in September 2025 forced a production suspension and phased restart, which cut near-term volume guidance.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Copper price (LME/COMEX), the dominant driver. (2) Gold price (significant by-product credit lowers unit costs). (3) Grasberg operational status and Indonesian policy (export permits, smelter, royalties). (4) US copper tariffs and the COMEX-LME spread. (5) China demand signals and global growth expectations.
- **Key numbers to check:** Copper and gold sales volume guidance, unit net cash cost per pound of copper, capex, net debt, sensitivity tables (EBITDA per $0.10/lb copper change), Grasberg ramp timeline.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings typically around the third or fourth week of Jan, Apr, Jul and Oct (before the open). China data (PMIs, credit, property). Indonesian government announcements. US trade/tariff actions on copper.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: structural copper deficit from electrification, AI data centers and grid build, with FCX holding large long-life reserves and a US production base. Bear: China slowdown, Grasberg operational risk and recovery delays, Indonesian sovereignty/fiscal risk, cost inflation, and tariff distortions.
- **Trading character:** High beta (above 1.3 historically). Earnings moves historically about ±3-7%. Strong momentum tendencies with copper. Sells off hard in risk-off and growth scares.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** Copper futures, COPX, SCCO, TECK, BHP, EEM/China proxies. Hedge with COPX or copper futures exposure. Copper/gold ratio signals.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: copper breaking out on China stimulus or supply disruptions, positive Grasberg restart news, pullback to support in an uptrend. Short: China data disappointments, copper failing at resistance, or a further mine disruption/guidance cut.
- **Verify live:** Grasberg recovery status and guidance, copper and gold prices, tariff regime on copper, net debt, next earnings date.

### NEM: Newmont
- **Business:** World's largest gold miner, expanded by the Newcrest acquisition (closed late 2023). Tier 1 portfolio across Nevada (Nevada Gold Mines JV with Barrick, where Barrick operates), Canada, Australia (Cadia, Boddington), Ghana, Peru, Papua New Guinea (Lihir) and others. It has meaningful copper by-product and has been divesting non-core assets.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Gold price, the main driver. (2) All-in sustaining cost (AISC) trends; costs have often risen with the gold price. (3) Production vs guidance and execution at key mines. (4) Capital returns (buybacks, dividends) and debt reduction. (5) Gold-miner sector flows (GDX), which have historically lagged the metal.
- **Key numbers to check:** Attributable gold production, AISC per ounce, free cash flow, net debt, buyback progress, reserve/resource replacement, divestiture proceeds.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings typically late Feb (annual), late Apr, late Jul, late Oct (after the close). Annual guidance update often in Feb. Fed meetings and CPI (real-rate moves). Central-bank gold purchase data (World Gold Council).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: gold structurally supported by central-bank buying and fiscal worries, so margin expansion translates into large FCF with the miner rerating vs the metal. Bear: cost creep and production misses (history of guidance disappointments), jurisdictional risk, a gold correction from crowded positioning.
- **Trading character:** Beta to gold historically 1.5-2.5x. Low or even negative correlation to SPY at times. Earnings moves historically about ±3-8% (execution surprises are common). Trends with gold; can mean-revert sharply after parabolic runs in the metal. In risk-off, often holds up if real rates fall, but sells off in liquidity-driven crashes (everything sold).
- **Correlated with / hedges:** GLD, GDX, AEM, GOLD (Barrick), WPM. Pair: long NEM / short GLD expresses miner-catch-up (or the reverse after cost misses). Hedge with GLD.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: gold trending up while miners lag the metal (GDX/GLD ratio depressed) and costs stabilize. Short: gold rolling over from a parabolic move, or after a cost/production miss when the stock underperforms GLD.
- **Verify live:** Gold price, AISC guidance, production guidance, divestiture status, CEO/management transition status, next earnings date.

## Utilities

### NEE: NextEra Energy
- **Business:** Largest US utility by market cap. Two pillars: Florida Power & Light (FPL), a large, fast-growing regulated utility in Florida, and NextEra Energy Resources (NEER), the largest developer and operator of wind, solar and battery storage in the US, with nuclear plants and a large contracted backlog. It also has a growing gas and nuclear angle, including a planned restart of the Duane Arnold nuclear plant in Iowa tied to a Google power agreement. Its affiliate yieldco (formerly NextEra Energy Partners, now XPLR Infrastructure) has been a source of negative sentiment.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Long-term interest rates (heavy capex, partly financed with equity and debt). (2) Renewable policy, including tax-credit phase-downs under the 2025 tax legislation and tariffs on solar components. (3) NEER backlog additions and data-center/hyperscaler deals. (4) FPL rate case outcomes. (5) Adjusted EPS growth guidance (historically targeted at a high single-digit compound rate).
- **Key numbers to check:** Adjusted EPS vs guidance, NEER backlog additions (GW), FPL rate base growth, credit metrics (FFO/debt), equity issuance needs, capex plan.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings in late Jan, Apr, Jul and Oct (usually before the open). Investor day events. Federal tax-credit and tariff policy changes. Florida PSC decisions. Hurricanes (storm cost recovery for FPL).
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: leading developer to meet surging power demand from AI and electrification, with FPL a premium regulated asset and data-center deals adding a new leg. Bear: policy risk on renewables, high interest rates raise financing costs, equity issuance dilution, and execution risk on new nuclear/gas.
- **Trading character:** Higher beta than typical utilities. Earnings moves historically about ±2-5%. More growth-stock behavior than bond-proxy (it has had large drawdowns on rate spikes and policy news). In risk-off with falling yields it can rally; in stagflation-style rate spikes it drops hard.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** XLU (largest weight), TLT, renewable names (FSLR, ICLN), CEG/VST on data-center days. Pair: long NEE / short XLU isolates the renewables/developer view. Hedge rate exposure with TLT shorts.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: yields falling, a policy overhang being resolved, NEER signing big data-center contracts while the stock is near support. Short: yields breaking higher, adverse tax-credit or tariff news, or equity issuance announcements.
- **Verify live:** Tax-credit policy status, EPS growth guidance, backlog figures, Duane Arnold restart timeline, dividend yield, 10Y yield, next earnings date.

### DUK: Duke Energy
- **Business:** One of the largest US regulated utilities. Electric operations in the Carolinas, Florida, Indiana, Ohio and Kentucky, plus gas utilities (Piedmont Natural Gas). A large nuclear fleet in the Carolinas. Very large multi-year capex plan focused on grid upgrades, new generation (gas and renewables), and data-center/industrial load growth in the Southeast. It brought in Brookfield as a minority investor in its Florida utility to help fund capex (announced 2025).
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Long-term Treasury yields (classic bond proxy). (2) Regulatory outcomes in North and South Carolina and Florida (allowed ROE, rate cases). (3) Load growth and large-load (data center, manufacturing) contracts. (4) Funding plan: equity needs, asset sales, minority stake sales. (5) Storm costs.
- **Key numbers to check:** Adjusted EPS vs long-term growth guidance (historically 5-7%), rate base growth, capex plan, FFO/debt, equity needs, dividend payout ratio, regulatory calendar.
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings early Feb, early May, early Aug, early Nov (before the open). Dividend increases historically announced in July. Carolinas integrated resource plan (IRP) decisions. Hurricane season.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: steady, visible rate-base growth with Southeast population and data-center tailwinds, constructive regulation, and a defensive yield. Bear: rising yields, rate-case pushback and customer-bill politics, heavy capex requiring equity, and storm/disaster exposure.
- **Trading character:** Low beta (about 0.4-0.6 historically). Earnings moves historically about ±1-3%. Mean-reverting and range-bound; it moves with yields more than with the market. Outperforms in risk-off when yields fall.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** SO, AEP, D, XLU, TLT. Pair: long DUK / short XLU to bet on regulatory catalysts; hedge rate exposure by shorting TLT or IEF.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: rates rolling over after a spike, stock at the low end of its range with an attractive yield spread vs 10Y. Short: yields breaking out, a bad rate-case decision, or a capital raise announcement.
- **Verify live:** Current dividend yield vs 10Y yield, EPS guidance, capex plan size, rate-case calendar, Brookfield deal status, next earnings date.

### SO: Southern Company
- **Business:** Large regulated utility holding company: Georgia Power (largest subsidiary), Alabama Power, Mississippi Power, Southern Company Gas, plus Southern Power (wholesale contracted generation). It completed Plant Vogtle Units 3 and 4 (the first new US nuclear units in decades) in 2023-2024 after major cost overruns and delays. Georgia is one of the most active data-center markets, with a very large large-load pipeline.
- **What moves the stock:** (1) Treasury yields. (2) Georgia PSC decisions and data-center load additions (Georgia Power IRP, certified capacity). (3) EPS growth guidance and capex upsizing. (4) Large-load contract terms (who pays for new capacity). (5) Rotation into defensive or AI-power names.
- **Key numbers to check:** Adjusted EPS vs guidance (historically targeting 5-7% growth), large-load pipeline (GW), capex plan, equity needs, FFO/debt, dividend growth (historically a long streak of increases).
- **Catalysts & calendar:** Earnings late Jan/early Feb, late Apr/early May, late Jul, late Oct/early Nov. Dividend increase historically in April. Georgia PSC elections and rate decisions. IRP filings.
- **Bull case / Bear case:** Bull: Southeast population growth plus data-center demand power rate base growth, Vogtle de-risked, supportive Georgia regulation. Bear: customer-bill affordability politics, data-center demand that doesn't materialize (stranded capacity), capex and equity needs, and rate sensitivity.
- **Trading character:** Low beta (about 0.4-0.6). Earnings moves historically about ±1-3%. Range-bound, rate-driven, with episodic AI-theme momentum. Outperforms in risk-off.
- **Correlated with / hedges:** DUK, NEE, XLU, TLT; AI power names on data-center headlines. Pair: long SO / short DUK on regulatory or load-growth differences. Hedge with XLU or TLT.
- **Long setups / Short setups:** Long: yields falling, data-center load announcements, stock near range lows with a supportive yield spread. Short: yields spiking, adverse PSC decision, or AI-capex scare hitting the data-center narrative.
- **Verify live:** Large-load pipeline, EPS guidance, Georgia PSC developments, dividend yield, 10Y yield, next earnings date.

## REITs

### AMT: American Tower
- **Business:** Large global tower REIT. Leases space on cell towers to wireless carriers in the US, Latin America, Europe and Africa (it exited India by selling its operations there, completed 2024). It owns CoreSite, a US interconnection-focused data-center business. Revenue is long-term contracts with built-in escalators; growth comes from carrier equipment upgrades (amendments) and new colocation.

### PLD: Prologis
- **Business:** World's largest logistics/industrial REIT. Owns and manages warehouses and distribution centers in the US and around 20 countries, with key positions near major consumption and port hubs. - **What moves the stock:** (1) Interest rates and cap rates. (2) Industrial supply/demand: net absorption, vacancy and market rent growth (oversupply from the 2021-2022 construction boom weighed on rents). (3) Trade and tariff policy (imports and supply-chain reconfiguration). (4) E-commerce and inventory trends. (5) Data-center pipeline monetization.
